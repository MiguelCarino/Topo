# Topo — feature reference

Everything Topo does, in detail. For what it is for and a short map of its features, see the [README](../README.md).

## Views

- **Simple** (`#simple`, or `#imagenology` for a medical imaging site) is for someone who is not a network technician: plain words, a short device palette, and only names and connections to fill in. A problem the checks find shows as a mark on the device. It is in English, Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil), Japanese and Russian.
- **The left rail.** With nothing selected, the library (Nodes, Templates, Blocks) and the panels below it (Configuration, Diagnostics, Subnets, Groups, Wi-Fi, Findings) share the rail. With a device, a cable or several selected, the library folds into one row and the panels take the whole rail; a click on empty map clears the selection and brings the library back. A click on the row keeps the library open whatever is selected, until the row is clicked again; that choice is kept in this browser.
- **Full editor** (`#advanced`) has every field, the Subnets, Groups, Wi-Fi and Findings tabs, the Table and Rack views, bulk editing and the spreadsheet round-trip. Its technical vocabulary (Gateway, DNS, VLAN, Interfaces…) stays in English on purpose, and so do the shipped blocks' names and the device names in blocks and templates; its sentences (the interface rows' tooltips and MAC notes, the blocks' descriptions, the trace's status line), the findings and the site report are translated, and a count of one reads in the singular ("1 link", "in 1 day").
- **Setup** (`#setup`) asks how many of each kind of device you have and draws a network around a router.
- **The map by touch** (Mobile batch 1). One finger on the map pans it, once it has moved past a tap; a tap still selects or deselects as before, and a pan never deselects. Two fingers pinch-zoom about their midpoint and pan with it, within the same limits as the zoom buttons; lifting one carries on as a pan. The zoom buttons, the document bar and the banners over the map count as map, so a pinch on them zooms the map too. On a Mac in Safari a trackpad pinch over the map zooms the map, as Ctrl-wheel does in other browsers. A gesture on the map never zooms the page (page zoom elsewhere is left to the browser), and the page has no pull-to-refresh, so a swipe down can't reload it and lose a network not yet kept (a touchpad's two-finger swipe back and forward is left alone). A finger on a device, a collapsed group, a frame's tab or a note still drags it; a second finger landing during that drag puts it back, saves nothing and pinches instead. A double-tap on empty map zooms in 2x about the tap and makes nothing; a mouse double-click still drops a note there (on a phone notes come from the palette). The panels, the drawer, dialogs and the table keep their own scrolling and text selection. On a touch screen How to use says how: drag with one finger, pinch to zoom, double-tap to zoom in.
- **The map's framing on a phone** (Mobile batch 1; phone width, 640px and under). Framing aims at the part of the map nothing covers: below the Map | Table switch and the search box, above the document bar, the banners and the handy bar. A network's first view (opening it, a template or the example, a Setup build, reloading, Back and Forward, and Tidy of the whole map) is framed with a small margin and never below a readable zoom (0.7, where device names are about 7px); a network too big for that is centred across with its top (the uplink or the router) in view, and the rest is a pan away. The **Fit** button (⤢, in the zoom control, everywhere) and F frame the whole network, and on a phone they, Zoom to matches, a pinch and the − button may go down to 0.1 so even the Full Showcase fits. 1:1 on a phone zooms to 1 about the middle of what is seen, and + and − zoom about it too. A map left below 0.2 when the screen widens past a phone's (a phone turned sideways) is never zoomed in by − or the wheel. A finding, a gateway jump or a table row brought into view comes in to the readable zoom on a phone, and Select members and the like come in to it when everything they show fits. Wider screens frame exactly as before.
- **Setup and Add devices on a phone** (Mobile batch 1). A Setup build and Add devices lay new devices out in rows sized to the screen: as many across as fit in the visible map at the readable zoom, beside the zoom buttons (4 on a 360 or 390px phone, never fewer than 3), each row centred under the switch or router it hangs off. A build of a couple of dozen devices then opens readable with the router at the top, and the rest is a pan down, rather than a strip of 12 at a postage-stamp zoom. A build's computers and other hosts start below every row of its switches and access points, so a short row that wraps never puts a host on top of one. When Add devices on a phone has to move the view to show what it added, it frames the new devices at the readable zoom or more, not the whole map. Wider screens keep rows of 12 at exactly the same places. A spreadsheet import still places in rows of 12.
- **Editing on the map by touch** (Mobile batch 2; 900px and under). A tap on a device, cable or note selects it as before, and a selection bar just above the handy bar names it and holds what a finger does with it, in 44px buttons: a device has Edit, Connect, Select more and Delete; a cable Edit, Select more and Delete; a note Edit text, Link (to a device), A− and A+ (its text size) and Delete; a set Edit, Select more and Delete; a read-only network Edit only. Edit opens the drawer at the item's fields without bringing the keyboard up; the drawer no longer has to be found by hand. Connect is the touch way to cable two devices that are already on the map: it arms link mode from the device and a banner says "Tap the device to connect to …" with Cancel; a tap on another device files the cable (one undo step), a tap on the same device, Cancel or a tap on empty map stops. Android's long-press on a device (a right-click) shows the same banner, except in a read-only network, which files no cable. Two fingers on either bar pinch the map, as on the document bar, and a quick second tap on Cancel never lands on the Delete that takes its place. Link on a note works the same way for its red leader. A double-tap on a note opens its text and no longer zooms the map. On a touch phone the map's little ✕ and A−/A+ discs are not drawn (they sat in the card's corner, where a finger aiming at the card deleted it); the bar holds them instead. Desktop right-click, L, the discs and every shortcut are unchanged, and link mode now shows its crosshair cursor as it was meant to.
- **Several devices selected by touch** (Mobile batch 2; 900px and under). A finger has no Shift, so the selection bar's Select more takes its place: while it is on, a tap on a device or a cable adds it to the set or takes it out, a tap on empty map or on a note's red leader keeps the set, a tap on a collapsed group's cable bundle adds its cables (or takes them out when all are in), a tap on a collapsed group's node or a frame's tab gathers the group's devices (the group stays collapsed), and a drag from a member moves the whole set (one undo step), a drag on anything else moves it alone with the set kept. A mouse or a pen at that width works the same way: a press that moves nothing is the tap, one that moves is the drag. The bar then says how many are selected and holds Edit (the drawer opens at the selection's counts, Select only devices/cables and, in full mode, the bulk panel), Delete (the whole set, one undo step) and Done, which keeps the selection and ends the mode. The mode also ends when the set empties, on an Esc that reaches the map (not one that closes a dialog or leaves a field), in the Table and Rack views and past 900px. Pinch and pan on empty map move the map as always. On a touch screen 900px wide or less the selection panel's hint reads "Tap devices to add or remove them." instead of the Shift-click one; a wider touch screen (a tablet in landscape), which has no Select more, keeps the Shift-click one. Desktop Shift/Ctrl/Cmd-click, the Shift-drag marquee and Ctrl+A are unchanged.
- **The drawer on a phone** (Mobile batch 2; 900px and under). The drawer scrolls as one column: a swipe anywhere in it moves it, with no box inside scrolling on its own (the library, the selection panel and a switch's port list are laid out at their full height; only text areas keep their own scroll), and nothing at its end is cut off. Its header, Controls with a 44px ✕ (Close controls), stays at the top, and the panel tabs (Configuration, Diagnostics, ...) stay just under it while their panel scrolls; a tab tapped deep in a long panel shows its own panel from the top, and anything the drawer brings into view (a port, a network's card, a Wi-Fi box) lands below the header and tabs, not behind them; Undo, Export and the other actions scroll away with the rest. The scrim still closes it too. On a phone the palette's Text and Note close the drawer first and put the new note in the upper middle of the uncovered map with its editor open, so what is typed is seen. A device from the palette keeps the drawer open for building a tree tap after tap (the library stays unfolded while the drawer is open, even when it is not kept open, and folds as usual once the drawer closes), and the drawer scrolls to the new device's Name (not focused, so no keyboard); when the drawer is closed, the map pans by the least it takes to show the new device. Above 900px the docked rail and the palette are exactly as before.
- **The menu bar on a phone** (Mobile batch 2; a touch screen 900px and under). The Carino menu bar at the top is put away so the map and the table get the whole screen; a short handle at the top edge shows where it is. A swipe down from the top edge (or from the Map | Table row), or a tap on the handle, brings it sliding in over the page, so nothing moves under it and the map does not pan. A tap anywhere else, a swipe up on it or a few seconds with nothing in it open put it away again; a tap in it starts those seconds again, and while its About window or a menu of its is open, a keyboard is on it or its handle, or a mouse rests on it, it stays. It does not come in under an open dialog, and a drag down in the Saved searches sheet scrolls the sheet. A pinch that starts at the top edge still pinches the map, and a double-tap there never drops a note. Turning a tablet across 900px with About open keeps About open and in view. Hidden, it is out of the Tab order and hidden from screen readers; Tab reaches the handle first, which shows it. How to use's Touch line names the gesture. Above 900px, and in a narrow window with a mouse, the bar stays pinned at the top as everywhere in the fleet.
- **The on-screen keyboard on a phone** (Mobile batch 3; a touch screen 900px and under). While a text field has the keyboard, the handy bar, the selection bar, the connect banner, the document bar and the read-only and storage banners step aside, so none of them sits over what is being typed. They come back when the field is left or the keyboard is put away. An open dialog fits the part of the screen the keyboard leaves, so its fields and its buttons stay in sight, and the field being typed in is scrolled into view inside its dialog, the drawer or the table; a phone held sideways (wider than 900px, 600px tall or less) gets the same fit and reveal. Otherwise above 900px, and in a window of any size with a mouse, nothing changes.
- **Typing on a phone** (Mobile batch 3). Each field asks for the keyboard its value needs. Addresses, subnets, MACs, VLAN lists, serials and dates get the full keyboard with no capitals and no autocorrect (a number pad has no '/', ':' or '-'); whole numbers get the number pad; a PoE budget and lengths the decimal pad (a PoE draw and a service interval keep the full keyboard, with no capitals, since they also take "class 2" and "1 year"); the search boxes a Search key; a device or network name is not autocorrected; a phone or email field its own keyboard. In the table on a touch screen, a cell being typed in says Next while there is a cell to type in below it: Enter keeps the value and opens that one, so a column is filled top to bottom without leaving the keyboard, and on the last such row it says Done and closes. A → at the end of the open cell keeps the value and opens the next cell along the row that takes typing, past read-only cells, ticks and picks. A value the column refuses stays open and marked either way. With a mouse and a keyboard, Enter and Tab work exactly as before.
- **Touch targets on a phone or tablet** (Mobile batch 4; any touch screen, and 900px and under). Every button, tab, menu item, link, tick and dialog ✕ takes a 44 × 44 tap, and fields are 44 tall as well as 16px. Ticks take their tap through their label or their table cell, so the box can stay small to look at. Table rows are 46 tall, the tick and twist columns 46 wide. A rack unit is at least 46 px tall on a touch screen at any zoom. In the full editor the menu bar's handle at the top edge stays a strip to swipe (it would lie over the search box); the drawer's header has its tap, ⌃, beside 🗂️ My networks and ✕. With a mouse at desktop width nothing changes.
- **Screen space on a phone** (Mobile batch 5; 640px and under, and a phone held sideways). The document bar shows in full once per network, then folds into a 44px chip (Keep, or the name once kept) after 6 seconds, a tap on the map or a selection, and steps aside under the selection bar. Held sideways, the drawer, the handy bar and the hidden menu bar apply as in portrait, the bars sit on one row, and the table and rack get their height back. The table's sub-tabs fade at an edge with more beyond it and scroll the chosen tab into view; the tick, twist and Name columns stay put while the rest scrolls. The rack has an Elevation / Patch schedule switch, each pane the full height. The search tools fold into the count and a ⋯ menu (Zoom, Select, Locate, Save), and the Map / Table / Rack switch shows glyphs. Address lines under devices hide below zoom 0.7 on a phone, except the selected device's. The Simple views open on the whole map when it fits at 0.33 or more, so a big network on a small screen opens whole. Provider mode opens on the Map with the report's devices ringed and in view. On a touch screen the landing says to open a file rather than drop one.

Switching views never loses anything; they show the same document.

## The register

- **Devices.** About twenty types (router, switch, firewall, access point, server, VM, PC, printer, IP camera, phone, imaging modality and more). Each device has interfaces with an address and a MAC, a gateway, DNS servers, open ports and an asset group: vendor, model, firmware / OS, serial number, asset tag, location, status (planned, active, retired) and notes. In the full editor a new device starts with nothing it was not given: no address, gateway, DNS server, model, allowed ports or NAT. Its address comes from the subnet it is cabled into, and its gateway and DNS from that subnet. Vendor and Model suggest what this map already holds (the models only the typed vendor's), and both are left out of the file when blank. Firmware / OS is the old "Model / firmware" field: a file from before keeps that text where it was, under the new label, and nothing is split by itself. **Split model and firmware** offers to move it: in the device editor, when this device's Firmware / OS text reads as a model, a line under the fields says "Model and firmware are in one field." with a **Split** button (its tooltip shows the result) that writes it as one undo step; and in the Table's Devices tab, **Split model and firmware…** (offered while any device has such text, not on a read-only network) opens a review with a tick per device, its text and what it becomes, all ticked, the ones it cannot read named under the list as left as they are; **Apply split** writes the ticked ones as one undo step. Text is split only at one spaced separator (` / `, ` · `, ` | `, ` ; ` or `, `) with a model on the left and firmware (a system's name such as IOS-XE, RouterOS or Windows, or a version such as 17.9.4) on the right, so "IOS-XE" and "Gi1/0" are never cut; a known maker's name first (Cisco, HP, Aruba, Synology…) goes to Vendor; a maker's model with no firmware and no separator in it ("HP LaserJet M404dn") moves whole and leaves Firmware / OS empty, while one with a system's name or version after it ("Dell OptiPlex 7090 Windows 11") or a note after a separator ("HP LaserJet M404dn, Floor 2") is left as it is; text that is wholly firmware already (a system's name with its versions and edition, such as "Windows Server 2019", or a type's default) stays, but a product line named like a system ("UniFi Switch 24 PoE / 6.5.55") is a model and splits. Only devices with no vendor or model typed are offered, nothing typed is lost (the words only move between the fields), and a device changed while the review was open is not written. **Vendor from the MAC**: while a device's Vendor is blank, the maker its interfaces' MACs name is offered as the field's grey text ("Dell · from the MAC") with a **Use** button beside the label that writes it as one undo step (not on a read-only network); the Table's Devices tab shows it greyed in the Vendor cell, with a tooltip saying it is not saved (editing or pasting into that cell starts from the blank the record holds). The hint follows the interfaces: typing, clearing or deleting a MAC or a NIC updates it at once. Nothing is written until Use is pressed or a vendor is typed, a typed vendor is never replaced, and the suggestion is not printed (not in the device list, nor in a printed saved search's Vendor column), exported or matched by `vendor:` (`mac:` still finds the device by its maker). It comes from the built-in table of about 35 makers (computers, phones and network gear; few printers, cameras or desk phones), and is not offered for a randomised or group MAC, a network-chip maker's MAC (Intel, Realtek, Espressif), a virtual machine's or container's (VMware, VirtualBox, Hyper-V, QEMU/KVM, Docker…), or when the device's MACs name different makers; an HP / HPE prefix reads HPE on a server, switch, access point or firewall and HP otherwise.
- **Retired devices** stay in the register, dimmed on the map. Nothing reaches them or passes through them, their own checks are skipped, and their address is free for a replacement without raising a conflict.
- **Subnets** (full editor, Subnets tab) are objects with an address, a name, VLAN, purpose, gateway, DNS servers and DHCP on or off, and show their size, use, next free address and members. Use counts every in-service device on an address in the subnet and the subnet's own gateway and DNS servers, drawn or not, so the free count is exactly how many addresses Add devices can still hand out; a retired device's address is free. The table's Subnets tab shows the same numbers in its **In use** and **Free** columns (read-only, for undeclared subnets too; an IPv6 subnet has no free count to show, and one the address-capacity note names is marked). With DHCP ticked, a subnet can record its **DHCP pool** (*DHCP first* and *DHCP last*, kept as typed); a pool that counts (both ends addresses of the subnet's family, inside its usable range, not reversed) adds a line to the card ("Pool 10.0.1.100–10.0.1.199 · 3 recorded of 100 · fixed 153 free": recorded means the devices, gateway and DNS written down here, never leases, which Topo cannot see), and the table gains *DHCP first*, *DHCP last*, **In pool** and **Fixed free** columns. Under the fields the card says what is wrong with a pool in the same words as the DHCP pool finding, and that a pool kept with DHCP unticked is not counted. When a subnet's pool counts, Add devices, bulk Move and a device dropped onto a switch keep the devices that hold a fixed address (routers, firewalls, layer-3 switches, edge and VPN gateways, load balancers, switches, access points, servers, printers, cameras and imaging modalities) out of it: each takes the lowest free address outside the pool, and only when none is left outside does it take one inside, which the preview says as a warning (a device dropped onto a switch, which has no preview, says it in a toast). PCs, phones, IoT, VoIP handsets, VMs, containers and custom devices still take the lowest free address, pool or not. With no pool, or one that does not count, addresses are handed out from the bottom of the subnet exactly as before. Blocks are moved to a free subnet as a whole, so they never land in a pool. A device belongs to a subnet by its address and inherits the subnet's gateway and DNS unless it sets its own; routers and firewalls keep their own. **Renumber** moves a subnet to another range in one step (its pool too, at the same host bits, as a block inserted onto a used range moves its subnets' pools; a pool end is never an address anybody holds, so it never refuses a renumber, and a pool that would not fit the new range is left as written for the DHCP pool check to point out) and refuses when the new range overlaps another subnet, is too small, or would put two devices on one address. Subnets in use that nobody declared are listed with **Declare**. Each subnet's zone can be drawn on the map: always, never or automatically for private ranges; a private subnet nobody declared is drawn automatically too. A zone is drawn round its own devices (a router on several subnets does not stretch them), and two automatic zones whose devices interleave are not drawn. In the full editor "subnet" is always one of these, and "network" the whole document.
- **Groups** (full editor, Groups tab) say where devices sit: a building, the floors in it, the rooms on them and the racks in those, or a group of your own. Each device is in at most one group, and it is also in every group above that one. The tab lists the tree with how many devices are in each group, and has Select members, Edit (name, kind, the group it sits inside, and a rack's size), + Inside and Delete. A rack's size is its height in units (4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24, 27, 42, 45, 47 or 48, or Custom…), its width (10″, 19″ or 23″, which the rack view draws it in proportion to, except that a 10″ rack is kept wide enough for a device's name to read), its depth in mm if you give one (it is printed, not checked) and its numbering: from the bottom, as most rails are, or from the top. The numbering only changes the numbers shown (on the rails, in a device's `U12`, its path, the findings and the report) and moves no device; the device editor's U and the place dialog take the number printed on the rails, which on a rack numbered from the top is the device's top unit. The spreadsheet's `rack_u` stays counted from the bottom either way, so a sheet means the same unit however the rack is numbered. A new height keeps every device on the units printed beside it: the units come and go at the top of a rack numbered from the bottom and at the bottom of one numbered from the top. Made shorter than a placed device, it asks first, naming the devices in the units it would lose; they then wait in the rack under Not placed yet. From the Groups tab or the rack view's Size, when the units left have room, the question also says where each would go and offers **Move them down** (up, on a rack numbered from the top): they all move by the same amount, keeping their order and the gaps between them, or, where something is in the way, each to the nearest free units on its face, still in order; one with no room left waits under Not placed yet. **Take them out of their units** leaves them all there, as before. Either is one undo step. That holds wherever the height is changed: the rack view, the Groups tab, a table cell, a pasted column (its preview counts the devices that would lose their place, and Apply asks) and an imported groups sheet (its review counts them). Deleting a group leaves its devices in no group, or deletes them too if you choose; the groups inside it move up a level. The device editor's Asset section has a Group picker, and for a rack the device's lowest unit, its height in units, the face it is on (front or rear) and a Full depth box, ticked by default: every device in a rack fills the rack front to back unless you untick it for a device on one face only (half depth), such as a patch panel or a PDU at the back. It also has **Ports face**, the side of the device its ports are on, which the rack view draws them on. A rack drawn before this default opens with all its devices full depth, so a device on the front and one on the rear that shared units are now an overlap; the finding says so and that both have to be set to half depth to share them. Where a device's location is shown (the table, the spreadsheet, the report, printed labels), a device in a group shows its group's path, such as `Building A / Server room / Rack 3 · U12–U13 front`. A device in no group shows its typed location as before. A network whose devices have locations typed in but no groups yet is offered **Make groups from locations**: it splits each location on ` / `, `>` or `,` into a tree, shows that tree first, and makes it in one undo step. The typed locations are kept as they were.
- **Groups on the map.** Each group with devices draws a dotted frame round them with its kind, name and device count on a tab. A group's frame sits inside the frame of the group it is in. A frame is not a subnet zone: zones are tinted and labelled with an address. The ▾ on a frame's tab collapses the group into one node that shows its kind, name and count, and the worst finding among its devices with how many there are. Cables with both ends inside a collapsed group are not drawn, and cables to the outside are drawn once per far end, marked ×N when there are several. Collapsing changes only the drawing: the checks, the table and the report still cover every device, and each device keeps its place, so expanding puts them back exactly. A click or tap on a collapsed node (or its ▸) expands it, and an expanded frame keeps the ▾ on its tab to fold it again. Dragging a collapsed node, or a frame's tab, moves the group's devices as one undo step; a press counts as a drag only once the pointer has moved a few pixels (more for a finger), so a tap never nudges the group. To select the group's devices, Shift-click the collapsed node, click an expanded frame's tab, or use Select members in the Groups tab (the way on a phone). **Drop in, drop out:** drag a device (or a selection) and let go inside a frame, and it goes into the innermost group under the pointer; let go outside every frame and it is in no group; let go on a collapsed node and it goes into that group, which stays collapsed. While you drag, the frames hold still, the target is lit and a label at the pointer says where the devices will go; a drag that ends inside the device's own frame only moves it. A group whose devices are all in the dragged selection travels with it and keeps them (so dragging the whole map with Ctrl+A changes no group). The move and the change of group are one undo step. Out of a rack a device leaves its units behind; into another rack it keeps them where they are free there, and otherwise waits under Not placed yet, which the message says; into a rack from anywhere else it has no units until you place it. **A new group from two devices:** drag a device (or a selection) and let go on top of another device's card, one in no group or in the group the dragged devices are all in, and Topo offers to make a new group of them: the device under it is ringed with a dashed green line and the label says "New group with …" while it is over it (a frame's target is lit in the accent colour, a host's in blue). The question asks the name and the kind, guessed from where it goes (a group of your own at the top, a floor in a building, a room on a floor, a rack in a room when every device is rack gear); the suggested name follows the kind until you type your own. Make group puts both in the new group, inside the group they share when they share one, with the dragged devices set beside the other rather than on it; Cancel only moves them, left where they were let go. Either way the move and the rest are one undo step. A device in another group is not offered (its frame takes the drop), nor one that runs on a host, nor two devices in a rack, which sit in units rather than in groups. Let go beside a card rather than on it, a device only moves. A finger drags a device on the map as the mouse does, with the same drops, once it has moved past a tap; a tap still selects. A finger drags a collapsed group, a frame's tab and a note as it does a device, past a tap the same way. The Group picker and Move to group do the same without dragging. Which groups are collapsed is kept in this browser per network. It is not part of the network, so it does not go into files or links. The search, the trace, Zoom to matches and a click on a table row reach devices inside a collapsed group through its node. The PNG, SVG and report diagram show groups collapsed or expanded as they are on screen. The simple views draw no frames.
- **Rack view** (full editor, Map | Table | Rack, shown while the network has a rack). A rack picker, a Size button with the rack's size fields (the same as the Groups tab's), and the rack's units numbered as its rails are, front and rear side by side (a Front/Rear switch on a phone). Each placed device is a box over its units with its icon, name and status. Each device shows its ports in a strip along its box, on the face they are on, evenly spaced in order (not where they are on the real faceplate): a switch's or access point's ports named as the box names them, 24 to a row, and another device's interfaces, a bond's members side by side under a bar. A free port is hollow, a cabled one is filled (in the cable's colour when it has one), one a finding is about (two cables on a port that takes one, past the faceplate's count, or a cable a finding names) is ringed, and one with an access VLAN is tinted. Several cables on a router's or firewall's interface are not ringed: like the findings, the rack view reads that as a gateway with a switch not drawn. A 1U box at 1× has no room under its name, so its ports are a hairline along its foot instead, one dash per port (faint when free, the cable's colour when cabled, red when a finding is about it), clear of the name. Which side of the device its ports are on is its **Ports face**: network gear (switches, routers, firewalls, access points, load balancers and the like) has them on its front and everything else on its back unless you say otherwise, in the device editor's rack section or with the Ports button beside the selected device's name; a half-depth device shows them on its own face, so for one of those the button and the field are greyed and say so. Point at a port, or tap one on the selected device, to read its name, label, VLAN and bond and its cable: the far device and port, the cable's tag, colour, length and via. The selected device's box grows over its neighbours so its ports can be read and tapped: its own units stay tinted inside it, and it steps in from the outer rail so the devices it is drawn over keep their edge and icon in sight there, where a tap selects them; it is drawn solid, over the cables, which come out under its ports; and the 1× / 2× / 3× switch makes every unit taller (kept in this browser, never in the network). Drag over empty units, or tap one, to add a device there. The dialog says where it goes in one line (Change opens the units, face and depth). **New device** is a grid of the types that go in a rack, server first, with an optional name and **Add**; **From the map** lists the devices already drawn, this rack's unplaced ones first, and opens first when there are some. Drag a device to move it, drag the top edge of the selected one to change its height, or use the arrow keys and the buttons under it. Each change is one undo step. Units that are taken are refused. A device takes its units on both faces (full depth, the default; its back is drawn on the other face) unless it is set to half depth with the Full depth button under it, the device editor's box or the place dialog's. The dialog's box is for a new device and is unticked by itself where only one face is free (worked out again as you edit the dialog's unit, height or face); a device already on the map keeps its own depth there (a half-depth one is marked so in the list), going in at half depth only where one face is all that is free, until you tick or untick the box yourself, which then sets the depth of whatever you pick. Where a device on one face and another's back are in the same units (an overlap), the back is drawn as a thin strip on the outer rail, so a click there always selects the device mounted on that face. Each cable is drawn from its port to the port at its other end: down to just under the box, along it at a level of its own (so a box's cables are separate lines, the one nearest the manager highest) to the cable manager at the side of that face (the column between the faces; the rear is drawn as the front's mirror, with its numbers on its outer rail), up or down the manager on a track of its own so lines do not stack (spread across the manager while there is room, so a few cables are a few clear lines; the two faces share the manager by how many cables each has there), and along the other device's lower edge into its port, in the cable's colour when it has one. A cable from a port on the front to one on the rear goes round through the manager. A cable to a device that is not placed in this rack runs to the manager too and on its own track up or down to the rack's top or bottom edge (the nearer), where it ends in a ring, so the cables from one device to several elsewhere are each a line of their own. A cable whose end has no port picked (or names one the device does not have) meets the box at its **no-port spot**, a dashed ring at the end of its ports; tap it to see those cables, each with **Pick a port**, which opens the port picker in the rail (one undo step), and the selected device's buttons have Pick a port too. A VM's or a container's cable meets its chip in its host's box. The **Cables** switch picks what stands out: **Selected** (the default) draws the selected devices' cables bold and tagged and the rest faint; **All** draws every cable, with their tags from 2× up; **One cable** traces one cable end to end: tap a port, a line or a tag. The choice is kept in this browser, never in the network (a patch schedule row switches to One cable for that visit only). A tag is a small flag hung under each port the cable leaves, with the cable's label, else its ports (`eth0 ↔ 24`), else where it goes (`→ PC-12 · eth0`); tags never cover one another nor another box's name, ports or chips, and a tag on show stays where it is while you point at it (one with no room near its port is left off; the port's tip still says it all). Point at a line or a device to light its cables and tags; tap a tag or a line to select the cable and show the port's tip: the tag, both ends, VLAN, length, colour and via. On a phone, which shows one face, a cable to the other face runs in the manager on its own track to the far device's level and ends in a ring there, tagged with the device, port and face there; the selected device's buttons sit in one row that scrolls sideways, so the rack keeps most of the screen, and a device tapped is kept in sight when they appear. On a phone or a narrow window the view scrolls as one column with the units stuck to its top (about a third of the screen or more): scroll down to Not placed yet or Other devices and their rows come up just under the units, ready to drag in; a device tapped then brings its buttons back into sight. Drag from a free port to a free port on another device to make a cable bound to those two ports, in one undo step (a finger drags from the selected device's ports). Let go on another device's box instead of a port, or drag from a device's dot to another device, or use Cable to…, and the far port is picked the way the map picks it, with the port picker open to change it. Selection is shared with the map and the table. To bring a device in, drag it from **Not placed yet** or from **Other devices** (every device not in this rack, with a filter) onto the units: it takes its own height there, and a device from elsewhere joins the rack. A finger drags a row by its handle; Place on a row does the same with a tap, at the lowest free units. A full-depth device brought in where only one face is free (behind a half-depth device) goes in at half depth, and a message says so. Near the top or the bottom of the units' pane, a drag scrolls it, so a device can be moved to units out of sight. To take one out, drag a placed device off the units onto one of the two targets that appear beside it (under the units, on a narrower window): **Not placed yet** keeps it in the rack without units, **Out of the rack** moves it into the group the rack is in (or no group). Let go anywhere else and it goes back. The selected device's Out of the rack button does the same with a tap. **Patch schedule:** beside the rack (under it on a narrower window) is the rack's list of cables, a row per cable that touches it: the device here and its port, the cable's tag (or "no tag", so the untagged ones the findings count are found at a glance), the far device and its port (and where it is, when that is not this rack), the colour, the length and the via. A cable between two devices of the rack is read from the higher one, and the rows run down the rack as it is read, a device's ports in order. Sort by any column with its head (again for the other way; blanks stay last); the sort is a view of this visit. Click or tap a row to select its cable (the rail shows it) and trace it in One cable mode, with the rack scrolled to bring its ends into sight; a cable selected on the rack lights its row. On a phone the schedule is the main view: the rack stays stuck above it as you scroll, and each row is stacked to fit, with the column heads as sort buttons. Patch panels, power, weight and device models are not drawn.
- **VMs and containers on their hosts** (full editor). A VM or a container says what it runs on with **Runs on** in its Asset section: a VM runs on a server (a hypervisor or a NAS is drawn as one, so servers are offered first), a workstation or a custom device, and a container on one of those or on a VM, one level deep; nothing runs on a container, and nothing on network gear, phones, printers, cameras or a cloud. A device with something on it lists what runs there, a VM's containers under the VM, and a name selects that device. A VM or a container is where its host is: it is in its host's group (its own Group picker shows the host's, greyed; let go into a rack with no host, the toast says to set its Runs on, not Open rack), moving the host into another room or rack takes it along, and in a rack it takes no units of its own (it is at the host's units, so it never overlaps one). Its place on the map and its cables stay its own. A host that is deleted, or a guest turned into another kind of device, leaves it running on nothing, where it was. **On the map**, drag a VM or a container (or a set of them) onto a device it may run on and let go: it runs there and is set in the nearest free place beside it (never on its card), in one undo step (the device is ringed and a label says "Runs on …" while it is over it, in a colour of its own, so it is never mistaken for a drop into a group or a new group, which a VM let go on a device it cannot run on is offered instead); a guest dragged anywhere else only moves. A finger does the same on a phone, and Runs on does it without dragging. A host carries a badge with how many run on it; a click or tap on the badge folds them into the host, drawn as a second card behind it, with their cables drawn to it once per far end (×N), and again brings them back. A folded host follows a collapsed group's rules: a click or tap on its card unfolds it (and selects nothing), a drag moves it, still folded, and Shift-click selects it; unfolded, a click selects it as any device. A fold lasts only while something runs on the host: when its last guest goes (the hosting undone, Runs on set to none, the guest deleted or turned into another kind of device), the fold goes too, so a VM dropped on it later is drawn beside it, and a redo brings the guests back unfolded. Like a collapsed group, folding is a view kept in this browser: the search, the trace, Zoom to matches and a table row reach a folded guest through its host, and Delete asks first. **In the rack view** a host draws what runs on it as chips in its box, a VM's containers after the VM, "+N" for those it has no room for (the place dialog offers no VM or container among its new device types for the same reason); a click on a chip selects that device (its bar has Select its host and Runs on nothing). Drag a VM or a container from Not placed yet or Other devices onto a placed device to run it there (it has no Place: units are not its to take, and let go on empty units, or on a device it cannot run on, it is refused, with a hint); drag a chip onto the Runs on nothing target beside the rack to take it off its host, where it waits under Not placed yet, or onto another device to move it there (held at the top or foot of the pane, the drag scrolls it to a host out of sight); a finger drags a chip too. A host's box with more than it has room for shows as many chips as fit, at least one, and "+N". A cable to a guest is drawn to its host's box, labelled with the guest's name.
- **Providers** (full editor, Providers tab) say who is responsible for what, so anyone new to the site, or from another company, knows whom to call. Each provider has a name, a service (Internet, Phone / VoIP, IT support, CCTV / alarm, Power / UPS, Hardware vendor, Cloud / hosting or Other), a contact person, a phone, an email, an account or contract number, support hours or an SLA, and notes; all optional but the name. Providers are set on a **device** (the modem, the PBX, the NVR), on a **cable** (the fibre circuit into the building) or on a **group** (a site, a room, a rack), several on one item since document v18 (the ISP and the IT firm on one router). There is no "main" one: a provider's role is its service. Inheritance is per service: what a device names covers the services it names, and for each service it names nobody for, its hosts answer (a container's VM, then the server), then its groups, innermost first, at any depth; a provider with no service counts as Other, so it never hides the group's ISP. A cable inherits nothing. The tab shows one card per provider with how to reach them and what they are responsible for ("Responsible for 3 devices · 1 cable"), with **Select what it covers**, **Report for this provider** (their part of the network as a printable report, PDF or HTML, a read-only network too), **Share their part** (a locked link of only their part, any time; see **What the link carries** below), Edit and Delete; deleting one leaves what named it with no provider, one undo step. Empty, it offers the usual kinds (Internet, IT support, Phone / VoIP, CCTV / alarm, Power / UPS, Hardware vendor, Cloud / hosting) one click each, and the kinds nobody provides yet stay under the cards as chips. A device's Asset section, a cable's panel and a group's card each have a **Providers** picker: the item's providers as chips, each with ✕ to remove it, and an **Add provider…** list of the others; a device says whose it is for the services it names nobody for ("From its group Server room: Acme IT · IT support", one line each), and the list's last entry, **+ New provider…**, makes one and adds it in one undo step. The bulk panel's Set fields adds one provider to many devices or cables, removes one, sets exactly one, or clears them, one undo step. The Simple and imagenology views say in plain words who looks after the selected device ("Who looks after this: Fibra Norte (Internet), Andes IT (IT support, through Office)"), translated. In the full editor's search, `provider:` finds what a provider answers for, by its name or its service (`provider:fibra`, `provider:internet`), and `has:provider` what anyone does; plain text finds a device or a cable by the name of a provider it names, never by an id. The report prints a **Providers** section on the technical sheet (how to reach each one, the contract, the hours and what they cover, a group's devices as one line), the provider under each device in the inventory and on each cable, and **Who to call** on the simple summary. An internet cloud (not one standing for another site) that nobody is named for, on the cloud, its group or a cable into it, raises an info note in the Findings tab; like an untagged rack cable it is listed but never counted, and the report leaves it out. With **Show the provider on hover** ticked in Settings, hovering over a device or a cable names its providers, one line each, how to reach them and through which group or host; it is off until ticked, and the exports never carry it. The spreadsheets carry them: `providers.csv` (found by `provider_id`, then by name), and a `provider` column in devices.csv and groups.csv and `cable_provider` in cables.csv, each naming the item's own providers separated by "; " ("Fibra Norte; Andes IT"); a name that holds a ";", or that two providers share, is written as its id, so the round trip stays lossless, and a sheet from before v18 naming one still reads. A name the map lacks refuses the cell for that row, so providers.csv comes first, as wifi.csv does. A merge treats an item's providers as a set: two-way their union, three-way each side's additions and removals, never a question; removing a provider the other side still uses stays a conflict. A block inserted from the menu brings no provider ids (they name the providers of the map it came from). A network holds up to 100 providers; none at all leaves no trace in the file. The full samples (Smart Home, Bank Branch, Data Centre, Hospital, Corporate Campus, Retail POS, Imaging Centre, Hotel, Warehouse, Call Center, Smart Factory, Media Studio, Vessel and the Showcase) come with made-up providers, serials, asset tags and in-service and service dates, chosen so none of them is past its expected life or due for service for years; the offline tiers, the military base and Common Errors are left as they were.
- **Wi-Fi.** A network keeps its Wi-Fi networks as a list of their own, like the subnets: each with its SSID, security (Open, OWE, WPA2, WPA3, WPA2/WPA3, the Enterprise modes, and WEP and WPA kept only so an old site can be written down), its bands (all of them unless it says otherwise), the subnet it puts clients on (and so its VLAN) and whether it is hidden. There is no passphrase field. A wireless interface's radio has a standard, shown by name with the IEEE letters (Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax), Wi-Fi 6E, Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be)), and the bands it works on; on an access point each band has a channel, a width and a power, and broadcasts every SSID on that band unless it lists its own. A new access point is Wi-Fi 6 on 2.4 and 5 GHz with channels on auto; any other radio starts blank, which means unknown. A wireless link records its SSID, band and signal in dBm; unless someone picks them, the SSID is the access point's only one, or, when it broadcasts several, the one whose subnet holds the client's address, and the band the best one both ends (and the SSID) have.
- **Wi-Fi in the editor** (full editor). The **Wi-Fi tab** sits next to Subnets and Groups whenever the network has an access point, a wireless link or a Wi-Fi network. It shows one card per network with its security, bands, subnet and VLAN, the access points that broadcast it and how many clients it has, plus **Select clients**, Edit and Delete. With no networks yet it offers **Staff**, **Guest** and **IoT**, one click each, and the ones not made yet stay under the cards as one-click chips. Staff goes on the subnet named for it, or on the only subnet there is; Guest and IoT go only on a subnet named for them. A **wireless link's panel** shows SSID, Band and Signal (dBm) where a cable's label, length, colour and via would be. Each select starts with what Topo works out ("Auto · Staff"), and Band lists only the bands both ends have. Under them is the access point's channel and width on that band, with **Edit radio**. A typed signal of 67 is read as −67, since a phone's number pad has no minus key. A VPN link has no cable facts either; any stored ones are kept but hidden. Under a wireless interface, the **radio** folds to one line ("Wi-Fi 6 · 2.4 GHz ch 6 · 5 GHz ch 36/80 · Staff, Guest"). Opened on an access point, it has the standard, a row per band with a channel and a width picked from the ones valid for the band (also for the bands that come from its standard, before any is ticked), and the SSIDs it broadcasts as chips. A client's radio has only the standard and bands; bands that come from its standard show greyed until changed.
- **Cables** carry a label, length, colour and what they run through (`PP-A:12 > WJ-3B`), since patch panels and wall jacks are not drawn as devices. A length is a number with its unit (m or ft) picked in front of it; a length an older file wrote in words (`about 3`) stays as written until you change it, in the table, the bulk panel and a spreadsheet round trip alike.
- **Addresses are read strictly**: four plain numbers for IPv4 (`192.168.1.010`, `10.1` and `3232235777` are not addresses, where a lenient reader would guess `.8`, `10.0.0.1` and `192.168.1.1`), and a prefix of digits only. Such text is kept as written and flagged as an invalid address. **Bonding** two NICs that carry different addresses asks which one the bond keeps.
- **Ports** (full editor). A switch or access point can name its ports the way the box does (`Gi1/0/` from 1 makes port 12 `Gi1/0/12`). A prefix cannot end in a digit. Each port can carry a label (the jack or desk at the other end), an untagged VLAN (an access port's, or a trunk's native one), its tagged VLANs (a trunk: `10,20,30-39` or `all`) and its PoE (802.3af, 802.3at (PoE+), 802.3bt Type 3 or 4, or a number of watts); a switch or access point can carry a PoE budget, any device a cable can power (not a cloud, a VM or a container) a PoE draw (watts, or a class: "class 4" is 25.5 W), and a wired interface its own untagged VLAN (else its subnet's) and tagged VLANs (a router on a stick, a hypervisor's uplink; a switch LAG's member ports read the LAG's when it has any, and a bond's members read the bond's). In the device panel each port's line in **Port labels, VLANs and PoE** has its label and its untagged VLAN, and a **⋯** that opens a second line with Tagged (red while it cannot be read, put back on leaving) and a PoE pick (—, the four standards, Watts…; a Watts… entry that cannot be read, or is left blank, keeps what the port had, and only — clears it); that line is open by itself on a port that has either. **Set on ports** at the top of the open list writes Untagged, Tagged or PoE on a run of ports (`1-24`, `1,3,5`, or the box's names; numbers are the box's own, so `0-3` on a `ge-0/0/` scheme counted from 0) as one undo step; a port the box does not have refuses the whole run, and a blank value clears it. The list's heading counts the noted ports, the trunks and the PoE ports ("Port labels, VLANs and PoE (12 · 2 trunks · 8 PoE)"), a dot marks every noted port on the faceplate, and a port's tooltip, the rack view's port tip ("Untagged 1 · Tagged 10,20", "PoE+ 30 W") and its tint read all of it. **PoE budget (W)** sits under the port count with a live line of what the devices on its PoE ports draw ("PoE: 47 of 120 W"; a retired device or one on a port that records no PoE is not counted); **PoE draw (W)** is under Gateway and DNS. **+ MAC / VLAN** opens, under each wired interface, its VLAN line: the untagged VLAN greyed with its subnet's ("Auto · 10 Staff", following the address as it is typed) until one is typed over it, and its tagged VLANs (not on a bond member, nor on a switch's port given a name in the list: those are the bond's and the port's); an interface that records either shows the line by itself. The link panel shows both ends' VLANs, read-only, once either end records one (an end with none "carries every VLAN"). The Table's Ports tab has Tagged and PoE columns (custom watts read "15.4 W", a standard "PoE 15.4 W"), the Interfaces tab VLAN (greyed when it is the subnet's) and Tagged (a dash on a radio, a bond member and a switch's port), all sorting by their numbers, and the Devices tab PoE budget (W) and PoE draw (W) once the network records any PoE; Change all sets both (a device that cannot hold one is skipped, saying why). Locate's step at the far port says which VLAN is untagged and which are tagged on a trunk. The VLAN facts are checked (**VLANs** under Checks) and so are the PoE facts (**PoE** under Checks); a port or interface with no VLAN recorded carries every VLAN, so a network that records none is judged as before. A port is **used** when a link lands on it or it is a bond or one of a bond's member ports (a LAG's ports are taken, cabled yet or not), and free otherwise, and that one count is what the device panel ("5 of 8 ports in use. 3 ports free.", or "Port count not set, so the total is a guess." while the faceplate's size was never set and so grows with what is plugged in), the report's inventory and port map, Add devices' next free ports and bulk Move to switch all read; a cable past a set port count is reported as one to rebind, and the overflow finding counts cables, not bonds; an access point's wireless clients are not ports.

## Checks

- Duplicate IP and MAC addresses, gateways outside the device's subnet or out of its reach, DNS servers it cannot reach, cables whose two ends disagree (IPv4 and IPv6), switching loops that need STP, a cable label or asset tag used twice, and notes that look like a password. For groups: groups that are each other's parents, a device past the top or below the bottom of its rack, two devices on the same units of the same face (or a full-depth device and anything behind it), and rack units on a device that is not in a rack. And, for information only, how many cables in each rack have no tag: listed in the Findings tab, but not counted on the pill, the tab, a card or the report, since nothing is wrong on the network. For VMs and containers: one whose host is retired, and one in another group than its host (which the editor never leaves; a file can).
- For Wi-Fi: a client on a band its access point's radio does not offer (or its own radio does not have), 6 GHz without Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 at both ends, a radio channel its band does not have or a width the band or the standard cannot take (these three on the radio itself mark the device), a client set to an SSID its access point does not broadcast (or not on that band), an SSID whose subnet does not hold the client's address, two access points on overlapping channels of one band in the same room or floor (with neither in any group, on the same subnet; two in groups but no shared room or floor, such as different buildings, are not flagged; the channel's width counts), an open, WEP or WPA (TKIP) network that an access point broadcasts (OWE is fine), and a signal weaker than −75 dBm, or −67 dBm for a VoIP phone. For information only, a 2.4 GHz channel other than 1, 6 or 11. What nobody filled in (a standard, a channel, an address, a signal) is unknown and never raises anything. Channels are compared between access points (and home routers) only, never a client's recorded channel, and a width the band cannot take counts as 20 MHz there. A wireless link between two access points (a mesh backhaul) has its band checked but is on no SSID, so its address is never tested against one. Security findings are one per network and overlap findings one per band, and accepting one is kept to that network or band: accepting Staff's WEP does not accept Guest turning open on the same access points, and accepting an overlap on 2.4 GHz does not accept one on 5 GHz. Otherwise one check on the same devices is one finding, so accepting it cannot quietly accept another.
- One set of checks feeds the marks on the map and, in the full editor, the device's Diagnostics tab, the **Findings** tab and the report, so they agree. The Findings tab groups findings by check and folds the same finding on many devices into one line; clicking a line selects the device and centres the map on it. A pill on the map counts what is open; A opens the tab and goes back.
- **Accept…** keeps a finding you know about, with a required reason and, optionally, a date: with no date it is accepted for good; with one it is set aside until that day, then comes back by itself the day after, saying what was scheduled. It stops counting everywhere (tab, pill, badge, saved card, report totals, bulk previews) and is listed under Accepted with its reason; Reopen takes it back. What is accepted is those devices (and, for an IP or MAC conflict, that address): a new device joining the same conflict, or the same devices clashing on another address, is open again. Deleting a device takes it off every accepted and pinned entry.
- A service finding (overdue or due) also carries **Serviced today** on its line, which records the service for every device on the line, in one undo step. **Pin** puts a finding on the list *To check on the next visit*, which heads the Findings tab, marks the device on the map with a pin and is printed in the report and the client's summary ("What is planned"). **Unpin** takes off every pin that covers the line, so the line is not left pinned by a second one.
- **Age and upkeep**: a device's *In service since* (a year will do), *Last serviced* and *Service every (months)*, typed or picked from a calendar. Its age and next service are worked out from today, never stored. Findings: *Age* (past the expected life of its type, or its own), *Service* (overdue; critical when missed twice over) and *Service due* (within 30 days, a note). Expected lives per type are Topo's usual unless the network sets its own (*By type…*). *Serviced today* records the service and clears its pins and schedules.
- **Firmware**: devices of one model (its vendor and model, without case; a blank vendor joins the model's one vendor) that run different firmware raise an advisory finding naming each version with its devices ("Cisco C9200L runs 2 different firmware versions: 17.9.4 on SW-1, SW-2; 17.6.1 on SW-3."), with no claim about which is newer. Versions are compared without case or spaces and without a leading v, version or fw ("v17.9.4" is "17.9.4"). Only devices in service or planned with both a model and a firmware typed count, so a file whose model and firmware are still in one field never raises it. Accepting it holds for those devices on that model, also when the model's one vendor is filled in or cleared later; one more device of the model raises it again, and so does a second vendor appearing under the same model. The simple summary says it as "Devices of the same model run different software versions".
- **Port capacity**: a switch or access point whose port count is set, with at least 4 ports, in service or planned, gets an info note when it is nearly out of free ports (at most one free, or a tenth of its ports: 1 of 8 or 16, 2 of 24, 4 of 48), naming the free ones as the box names them ("Core has 1 free port of 8: 8."), and a note in its own words when none is free. A port is used as the Ports bullet above says (a bond takes its ports). A box whose count was never set grows with what is plugged in, so it never gets one; one already over its count gets the critical overflow finding instead, never both. Like an untagged rack cable, the note is listed in the Findings tab but never counted (no pill, no badge, no total, not in the report's findings); it can be accepted, pinned or scheduled, and an acceptance holds for that port count only, so changing the count raises it again. `has:full` finds those switches.
- **Address capacity**: an IPv4 subnet of at least 8 usable addresses (so not a /29 or smaller, which is a transit link) gets an info note when it is nearly out of free addresses (at most two free, or a tenth of the subnet rounded up: 26 of a /24, 2 of a /28), counted as the Subnets bullet says (its devices and its own gateway and DNS, drawn or not), with its own wording when none is free ("10.0.9.0/28 has 1 free address of 14."). Every subnet the Addressing table lists is checked, declared or only implied by an address; IPv6 never is. Like the port note it is listed but never counted; it names the subnet's in-service devices, so an acceptance reopens when another device joins it, and it is accepted per subnet. With a DHCP pool that counts, it measures the fixed addresses outside the pool ("10.0.1.0/24 has 4 free addresses outside the DHCP pool, of 154."), and a subnet the pool fills completely, or leaves fewer than 8 fixed addresses (the router at .1 and a pool .2–.254), gets none: that is full by design. Topo says nothing about how full a DHCP pool is: it cannot see leases.
- **DHCP pool**: an advisory (counted) on a subnet with DHCP on whose pool does not count (one end missing, an end that is no address, the wrong address family, outside the subnet, taking in its network or broadcast address, or ending before it starts), or whose own gateway or a DNS server lies inside the pool, the classic way a server hands out the router's address. It names the devices holding those addresses when they are drawn, else the subnet's devices, and is accepted per subnet and per problem ("excluded on the server"), so a pool that later turns reversed or outside the subnet is raised again. A pool with DHCP unticked, or on a subnet whose own address does not read, raises nothing. The simple summary says it as "The automatic address range clashes with a fixed address".
- **PoE**: judged only from what is recorded. A device with a PoE draw cabled into a port that records no PoE, on a switch or access point that records a PoE budget or any PoE port, is critical (*PoE power*: "Cam 2 draws 6.49 W over its cable, but SW · 2 gives no PoE."), unless another of its cables lands on a PoE port; on a switch that says nothing about PoE, a router's NIC (an injector nobody drew: Accept it) or over a radio it is never judged. A device whose draw is more than its best PoE port gives at the device is advisory ("Cam 1 asks 25.5 W; SW · 7 is 802.3af, 12.95 W at the device."; a device of class 0 or class 3, held as 13 W, is what such a port is for and is not flagged; a custom port is compared on its own watts, which are watts at the switch, since Topo cannot know what the cable loses). What a switch's PoE ports are asked for over its budget is critical (*PoE budget*: "SW: 134 W drawn of a 120 W budget (8 devices)."), and 90% to 100% of it an info note listed but never counted, like the port note. A device cabled to the PoE ports of two switches counts in full against each budget, since either may have to feed it. Retired devices, and a cloud, VM or container, are not judged or counted. Accepting the budget finding or the note holds for that budget and those devices: a new budget, or another powered device, raises it again. The simple summary says them without the word PoE ("A switch is asked for more power than it can give"). The Full Showcase records PoE on two switches with no finding.
- **VLANs**: judged only from what is recorded, and a port or interface that records no VLAN carries every VLAN, so a network with no VLAN facts gets none of these. Three findings. *VLAN path* (critical): a device whose gateway check is good (the checks' reachability walk is VLAN-blind, whatever the Trace's VLAN tick says) but whose VLAN does not get to its gateway over the switches, managed or not, nor access points: "VLAN 20 does not reach its gateway Core L3: Switch A · 24 ↔ Core L3 · 1 does not carry it. On it: PC-1, PC-2." The device's VLAN is the untagged VLAN of the switch port it is plugged into, else its NIC's (typed on it, else its subnet's); a Wi-Fi client's is its SSID's subnet's, walked from its access point. The walk crosses a cable when both ends carry the VLAN (untagged, tagged or `all`); an interface whose only VLAN is its address's subnet's (nothing typed on it) lets every VLAN through, so a router drawn with one cable for several subnets is not flagged. The cable named is the one that alone keeps the VLAN out (a trunk before an access cable set up for another VLAN, so a router with one leg per VLAN is not blamed on the wrong leg), else the first on the way that does not carry it; devices stopped at the same cable for the same VLAN are one finding, and accepting it holds for that VLAN and those devices (a VLAN 10 break on the same cable, or a new device behind it, is raised again). *VLAN mismatch* (per cable): a device plugged into an access port of another VLAN ("PC-3 is on Staff (VLAN 10) but plugged into Switch A · 5, which is VLAN 20.") or two ports with different native VLANs is critical; a VLAN tagged at one end only, or tagged at one end and sent untagged at the other, is advisory, listing them; an interface with nothing typed on it (its VLAN only its subnet's) takes no part in that tagged comparison, and is not called plugged into the wrong port when the port carries its subnet's VLAN tagged (an access point or a router on a stick with nothing typed on its uplink); radio links, VPN tunnels and a VM's bridged or passthrough link are not judged. *VLAN subnet* (advisory): an access port's untagged VLAN that no subnet has, once per VLAN, naming the ports ("VLAN 30 is untagged on SW · 7, SW · 8, but no subnet has it (2 ports)."); a tagged VLAN (a transit VLAN) and a trunk's native VLAN are left alone. Retired devices take part in none of them. The simple summary says them without the word VLAN ("Part of the network cannot reach its way out", "A device is plugged into a port set up for another network"). The Full Showcase's Studio uplink is a trunk carrying VLAN 20 tagged at both ends, with no finding. A file that wrote an access VLAN on an uplink port as a label may now get a VLAN path or VLAN mismatch finding: clear the port's VLAN, make it a trunk, or Accept it. A file that records access VLANs on its ports but no VLAN on its subnets gets one VLAN subnet advisory per such VLAN: set the subnet's VLAN, or Accept it for that VLAN. The interface's greyed Auto VLAN and the Interfaces tab's VLAN column show no untagged VLAN on an interface that tags its own subnet's VLAN (an SVI on a trunk), as the checks read it.
- **Trace** (full editor: Diagnostics tab, or T) shows everything the selected device can reach, optionally through one port. A switch or an access point passes traffic on whether or not it has a management address: the address only makes it a device on that subnet as well, so it is reachable itself from devices on that subnet (not from another subnet nothing routes to, and not through NAT from outside) and can be the trace's start (walking from it as a device on its management subnet). With a trace port, a switch on the path keeps the links through it lit; asked about itself, a switch with an address is judged on its own Allowed Ports (so is a DNS server set to its address), while one with no address is transit. A retired switch passes nothing. The gateway and DNS checks read the same walk, so a device behind a managed switch or a Wi-Fi client of a managed access point finds its gateway and DNS server. A ring of managed switches is not reported as an L2 loop (a managed switch is where STP runs); a ring of switches with no address is. **VLAN** (the tick beside Port filter, on by default, never saved: every visit starts with it on): the trace follows one VLAN at a time. It starts on the device's own VLAN, the untagged VLAN of the switch port it is plugged into, else its interface's (its own or its subnet's); a Wi-Fi client starts on its SSID's subnet's VLAN; a managed switch on its management address's. A cable is crossed only when both its ends carry that VLAN, and a router or another device is reached only through a cable that carries it, so a camera on VLAN 20 behind a trunk that lacks 20 reaches the other cameras on its switch and not the router, and a PC on Staff plugged into a VLAN 20 port reaches neither its gateway nor Staff. A managed switch or access point on the way passes the VLAN's traffic (its cables light) but is itself reached only when its management address is on a subnet of the VLAN walked (or of no VLAN). A router walks each of its subnets on that subnet's VLAN, so it still routes into VLAN 20 from VLAN 10 when its own trunk carries both. An end with nothing recorded carries every VLAN (so does an interface whose VLAN is only its subnet's), and a device's subnet with no VLAN is walked VLAN-blind, so a network with no VLAN facts traces exactly as with the tick off. A router's subnet with no VLAN (an undeclared WAN or LAN) is walked untagged: across ends that record nothing, access ports and trunks with a native VLAN, but not across a trunk that lists only tagged VLANs, so a WAN address does not light the VLANs its trunks lack. A retired start walks nothing and names no VLAN. The status line names the VLAN ("→ 5 devices · VLAN 10"; a router lists its subnets' VLANs, "VLAN 10,20"). Unticked, the trace walks every VLAN, as before. The checks never read the tick: the gateway and DNS checks stay VLAN-blind and a VLAN that does not reach its gateway is the VLAN path finding's. Spanning tree, VTP pruning, private VLANs, QinQ, a voice VLAN learnt by LLDP and a tagged native VLAN are not modelled.
- **Locate** (full editor: the Locate section in a device's panel, after Asset, or W) shows where the selected device is physically plugged in, worked out from what is recorded and never stored: its NIC, the cable (tag, colour, length, and the via exactly as typed), the switch or AP port with its jack label and VLAN (untagged and tagged on a trunk), an open *VLAN mismatch* or *PoE power* finding on that cable in the Findings tab's words (gone once accepted), that box's rack units and its groups (building, floor, room, rack). A VM or container starts from the host it runs on; a bonded or dual-homed box shows one path per cable. Over Wi-Fi it goes on from the access point to the switch port the access point is cabled into, and behind a phone to the phone's switch port. What the register does not hold is a muted line, never a guess: no cable, no port picked at the switch (with Pick a port), no jack label, no via, in no group (with the location typed on it), not placed in its rack, which side of a radio link is the access point, which access point is upstream, a retired device (dimmed; the walk goes on). Every step is a button: a device or cable step selects it, Open in rack shows the rack tracing that cable (offered only on a cable the rack view draws, so never on a radio link), and Show on map selects the whole path and fits it in view (from the table or the rack view it switches to the map, which stays the chosen view like the view switch; on a phone it shuts the drawer first); none of it is an edit or an undo step. The section's open state is kept in this browser. With nothing selected, W focuses the search box as / does. When a search matches exactly one device (by name, `ip:`, `mac:` with any separators, `tag:` or plain words), the search tools gain Locate, which selects that device and opens its Locate section; on several matches the button is disabled with "Narrow the search to one device", and Topo never picks the first. It is offered on the map, the rack view and the table's Devices tab. Opening a printed asset label's link (`#asset/<tag>`) in the full editor opens the device's Locate section as well as selecting it. The simple views (`#simple`, `#imagenology`) show the same answer as one read-only plain sentence in the device panel, one short sentence per step, with the switch's port called a socket and its jack label the wall socket, and the groups by name only: "Plugged into Office switch, socket 3 (wall socket Reception 2). Office switch is in HQ › Server room.", "On Wi-Fi through Living AP. Living AP is plugged into Office switch, socket 4.", "Not plugged into anything yet." It never shows the via, the VLAN or the rack units, and a missing link is said in plain words ("Which socket is not written down."). A VM or container names its host as what is plugged in ("Runs on HST. HST is plugged into Office switch, socket 6."). Between two access points, or an access point and a router, it never says which one serves the other: "On Wi-Fi with Root AP. Which access point is upstream is not written down." A retired device on the way is said too ("Old switch is retired."). W shows it there, and so does opening an asset label's link. On the simple summary, a device's Where cell gains the socket it is plugged into by cable when the register holds one ("HQ / Reception · Office switch, socket 3 (Reception 2)"), a retired switch is marked ("Old switch (Retired), socket 3"), and a device with a socket but no place gets the Where column too; nothing is added for Wi-Fi, for a cable with no port picked, or for switches, access points and routers, so a network with no sockets recorded prints exactly as before. Print, in the Locate section (that device), and Print where they are plugged in, on the rail for a selection that holds devices (a mixed set prints its devices; not offered for cables only), open a print preview of one self-contained page to take on site: the network's name and the date, then a block per device by name with its asset tag, address and MAC, every cable's steps (NIC, cable, via as typed, port with jack and VLAN, rack units, groups) and every gap and stop in the same words as the section, with no buttons on paper. It fetches nothing, so it prints offline; on screen it follows a light or dark system theme, and on paper it is always black on white with no device split across pages. The preview shows the page as it prints, on A4-wide paper with a dashed line where each A4 page ends (the status says how many pages; on Letter they end slightly elsewhere), scaled down on a phone rather than reflowed; **Print** prints it, **Download** saves it (`topo_<organization>_<network>_<date>_locate.html`), and **Close**, Esc or a click outside saves nothing.

## Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi is recorded where it lives: the networks as a list of their own (the Wi-Fi tab), the radio on each wireless interface, and the SSID, band and signal on each wireless link (see The register above). Most of it fills itself in: a new access point is Wi-Fi 6 on 2.4 and 5 GHz, a link takes the access point's SSID and the best band both ends have, and only what differs needs typing.

- **On the map** (full editor) a wireless link carries its band ("5 GHz") as a small chip on its own line, toward the client end and placed clear of subnet names, devices, other chips and a selected link's ✕, so it is always plain which link it belongs to; its tooltip is the SSID and the signal, and a tap on it (or just round it) selects the link. A 6 GHz link is drawn in a finer dash of the same purple with a filled chip, so a Wi-Fi 6E or 7 association stands out. The purple is a token (`--topo-wifi` in css/app.css). Tracing still sends the ))) arcs down every radio link.
- **In the report**, a Wi-Fi section after Connections: a table of the networks (SSID, security, the bands it is broadcast on by its access points, else the bands it is set to, subnet and VLAN, the access points broadcasting each and its clients), then a block per access point with its standard and a row per band of each of its radios (an access point with one radio per band shows each, named by its interface): channel and width ("auto" when left to the access point), the SSIDs on it and each client on it with its signal in dBm. A network with nothing wireless has no Wi-Fi section.
- **The starting points** have real Wi-Fi with no findings: the Smart Home's Wi-Fi 6 access point broadcasts Home and an IoT network on 2.4 GHz for the camera and the printer; Retail has a guest network on its own subnet, the Hotel one guest network on both floors' access points, and the Showcase's office a Wi-Fi 6E access point whose 6E laptop lands on 6 GHz by itself. Under Examples, the Campus's visitor centre has a guest network (OWE) on its own subnet, the Warehouse's handhelds a Scanners network on WPA2-Enterprise, the Vessel a crew network and the Military Base a motor-pool network on WPA3-Enterprise, each with its radios, so every band shown is the one those devices would really use. Common Errors adds one Wi-Fi fault to find: an open guest network.

## Working with many devices

- **Subnet colours.** Each subnet is tinted, outlined and its name tab written in its own colour from eight; neighbouring addresses (10.10.10.0/24, 10.10.20.0/24…) get different colours, a subnet keeps its colour from one redraw to the next, and none repeats until all eight are in use. The dark and blueprint maps use a light version of each that reads on black and on blue; the light map and the exports use a darker version of the same colour.
- **Tidy cables and labels.** A cable that would run straight through a device it does not connect to bows round it, on the side that needs the smaller bend, as long as a modest bend clears the way (a long cable that would have to swing wide stays straight); several cables between the same two devices fan out round it together. Cable labels and a subnet's name tab are drawn over the cables, so no cable crosses them, and a cable label moves along its cable off any device, note, tab or other label in the way. Dragging a device also drags the notes whose red lines all point at what is being dragged, as one undo step; a note that also points at a device staying put stays. Device names and addresses have a halo too, so a cable leaving a card passes behind its own name.
- **Subnet zones, read cleanly.** A network whose devices are all routers, firewalls or load balancers (a transit link) draws no bubble unless set to draw. A network whose devices are in different groups gets one bubble in each group instead of one stretched over everything between them, and its gateway alone in another group gets none. A zone's name tab sits on its top edge where no device, name, group tab or other zone tab is.
- **Hover.** With a mouse, the device, cable, note or collapsed group under the pointer lights up in the accent colour, so it is plain what a click will pick. It is only a look: nothing is selected, no panel opens and the map is not redrawn, and exports never show it. A finger has no hover, so a phone shows none.
- **Selection.** Shift- or Ctrl-click adds or removes a device or cable, Shift-drag boxes devices in, Ctrl+A takes everything, and a subnet's or a group's **Select members** takes everyone in it. A selection moves, deletes and undoes as one step.
- **Change all of them** (full editor, two or more devices): set a field on all of them (a field they differ on says "mixed"; blank leaves it alone; Clear empties it so the subnet's value applies again; under the fields, **Fill vendor from MAC**, offered while one of them has no vendor, fills each blank Vendor with the maker its MAC names and lists the rest with why: already has a vendor, kept; randomised MAC; chip maker; virtual machine; maker not in the built-in list; MACs that disagree; no MAC); rename them with a pattern such as `CAM-{n:02}` or find and replace; move them to a subnet (each takes the next free address; on a subnet with a DHCP pool the fixed-address devices step past it: the preview says so under its title, names them in a plain line and on each one's row, and warns only for one that found no address outside the pool); move them to a group (a device leaving a rack leaves its units behind; a VM on a host stays with its host, and a host's VMs go with it); put their VMs and containers on a host, or on none (a VM on a VM is refused on its row); set the SSID and band of the ones on Wi-Fi (**Wi-Fi**, offered only when the set has a device on Wi-Fi; Auto takes them back to what Topo works out, and a band the two ends do not share, or an SSID the device's access point does not broadcast, is refused on its row); re-cable them onto another switch or access point (each moves to the next free port, keeping its cable's label and its own address); or delete them. Every change shows first what would change and what the checks would find afterwards (for example "+2 Duplicate IP, −24 Gateway"), then applies as one undo step. Selected cables get the same treatment for their own fields.
- **Map | Table** (full editor): the same network as rows on six tabs (Devices, Interfaces, Cables, Ports, Subnets, Groups), a seventh, Wi-Fi, whenever the rail offers its Wi-Fi tab, and **Inventory** whenever a device has a vendor, a model or a firmware / OS typed. Inventory counts the devices three ways, picked with **By vendor**, **By model** and **By firmware / OS** above it (a choice for this visit, not saved): a row per value with how many devices are in service or planned, how many are retired (counted apart) and their names, the devices with none in one muted **Not recorded** row at the end. Vendors and models are compared without case or extra spaces and shown in the spelling most of them use; a model with no vendor joins its one vendor (apart when it is recorded under two), and on By model each model lists the firmware versions of its devices in service with their counts ("v17.9.4" and "17.9.4" are one), marked as a finding while its firmware finding is open. A vendor the MAC only suggests is not counted. Its rows are read-only: clicking one selects its devices, and ticking several (or shift- or ctrl-clicking) selects all their devices together, so **Change all of them** edits them together; sorting by a value column keeps Not recorded last; the search keeps a row while one of its devices matches; **Split model and firmware…** is offered here too. With Wi-Fi, Interfaces also shows each radio's standard, bands and channels, and Cables each wireless link's SSID, band and signal (greyed while they are worked out rather than set). A wireless or VPN link shows a dash for the cable's facts. The Devices tab has a Group column that takes a group's path (greyed on a VM on a host, which is where its host is) and a Runs on column that takes the host's name or id on a VM or a container; the Groups tab has a rack's height, width, depth and numbering. Edit a cell in place; a value that is not valid for its column is refused. On a touch screen a tap on a cell selects its row and a second tap on the same cell edits it, which a line above the table says in place of the mouse and keyboard hint (not on the read-only Inventory tab or a read-only network). Sort by any column, move with the arrows and Tab, and click a row to find it on the map. The first column (Name on Devices, the device on Interfaces, an interface's name folded open under its device) stays pinned at the left while a wide table scrolls sideways, and a cell the cursor moves to is scrolled out from under it. **Paste a column** lays a spreadsheet column down the ticked rows, or from the cursor's row downward, with a preview first. **Columns…** on the Devices tab shows, hides and reorders the columns (Name stays first), and adds the network's own: text, number, date or yes/no, renamed, given another kind or deleted there (a change that would lose values asks first). The bulk panel sets them on many devices at once, the site report prints the ones the table shows on a line under each device, and Changes since the last back-up lists them by name. The arrangement and the columns are saved with the network, so they open the same on another machine; each change is one undo step. It is only a view: devices.csv keeps Topo's columns in their order, then the network's own in the order they were made, so a sheet always reads back. On import, a heading Topo has no field for can be made a new column (its kind guessed from its values), and a heading that names one of the network's columns fills it. On a phone the table opens first.
- **Search** (full editor) works on the map and the table: plain words match any field (and an SSID finds the devices broadcasting it or on it), and `type:`, `ip:` (an address or a network such as `10.10.30.0/24`), `mac:`, `vlan:` (an interface's own VLAN or its subnet's, a port's access VLAN, or a tagged VLAN either carries, `all` carrying every one), `switch:`, `status:`, `loc:`, `group:` (a group's name or part of its path, matching the devices in it and in the groups inside it), `host:` (a host's name or id, matching what runs on it and on its VMs), `tag:`, `vendor:` (or `make:`; only what was typed, while `mac:dell` finds a Dell NIC by its address), `model:`, `fw:` (or `firmware:`, `os:`; the Firmware / OS text), `ssid:` (an access point that broadcasts it and the devices on it), `band:` (`2.4`, `5` or `6`), `wifi:` (a standard: `wifi:6e`, `wifi:ax`, `wifi:7`), `channel:` and `has:` (`finding`, `group`, `vendor`, `model`, `fw` (or `os`), `host` for what runs on a device, `guests` for a device something runs on, `wifi` for a radio or a wireless link, `full` for a switch or access point nearly out of free ports, `trunk` for a port or wired interface with tagged VLANs and the device holding one, `poe` for a PoE port, a PoE budget or a PoE draw, `history` for a device with any past value in this browser's address history, and more) and `was:` narrow it; `-` leaves a word out. `was:` finds a device on the map by a value it held at an earlier back-up or report and holds no more (see **History**): an address (`was:192.168.1.20`, not the device that has it now), a network (`was:10.0.0.0/24`) or the start of one (`was:192.168.`, `was:2001:db8`), a MAC with any separators or none, or text of the switch port it was on (by the switch's name at the time), its group's path then (`was:"rack 3"`) or its name then (a name or group that looks like a MAC or an address fragment, such as `DC-01` or `A-1`, is found as text too); an address moved to another of its NICs or whose prefix length alone changed, a switch renamed (even for a device cabled to two switches) or a group renamed is no past value, for `was:` and `has:history` alike. The line naming a removed holder answers only a search of `was:` (and `has:history`) terms, each matched by any of its past values, since nothing else about a removed device can be asked; and a back-up, report or forgotten history redraws a `was:` or `has:history` search at once. When a removed device held the value, the line under the box says so ("Also held by removed device CAM-03 until 2026-09-14.", three named and the rest counted), since it cannot be lit on the map; on a network not kept in this browser, or a read-only one, `was:` and `has:history` are understood, match nothing, and the line says no history is kept for this network. A word it does not understand is reported under the box. **Zoom to matches** and **Select matches** act on the result. **Saved searches**: ☆ **Save search**, the last of the search's tools, keeps the search under a name in the network (it travels in the file, the link and the binder), with the Devices columns to print in their order (or as the Devices table shows them), a sort with Descending, and whether it is marked to print in the site report (see **Site report**). The dialog parses the search as it is typed, with the same "Not understood" line, and nudges a type name typed in another language toward its key (`type:camera`), which matches in every language. It is offered only on a search Topo understands, and never on one that reads what this browser alone remembers (`was:`, `has:history`), since it would match differently on another computer. While the network has saved searches, **★ Saved ▾** at the end of the box lists them, each with its live device count and 🖨 when it is marked to print: a click on the name puts its search in the box (the map dims and the table filters as if it were typed), and Edit and Delete (which asks first) change it. When the box holds a saved search, ☆ reads ★ **Update saved search** and edits that one. Each save, edit and delete is one undo step, a read-only network refuses them (running one still works), there are at most 30 per network, a search of at most 500 characters printing at most 20 columns (Save says so past either, and when every column is unticked without "As the Devices table shows them"), and an edit keeps a sort or a stored column the dialog does not list. The simple views show none of it. On a phone the list is a sheet under the bar and the tools card wraps onto more rows.
- **Add devices**: count each kind, give a name pattern (`Phone-{n:02}`, numbered after the names already on the map) and choose the switch or access point. The new devices take its next free ports and the next free addresses of its subnet, inherit the subnet's gateway and DNS, and arrive selected as one undo step. The dialog says first which names, ports and addresses they will get (on a subnet with a DHCP pool, which devices were kept out of it and at which addresses, one line per pool when an access point's own subnet and its Wi-Fi network's subnet each have one), warns when they would not fit on the switch, and warns when a pattern without `{type}` would number every kind in one run. Devices joining an access point by radio join its Wi-Fi network. When it broadcasts more than one, the dialog asks which, starting on the first that is not a guest network, and the devices take their addresses from that network's subnet. A switch on a router port with no address gets no addresses from the router's other ports. Started from the landing, it builds a new network around a router whose LAN every switch is cabled to.
- **Blocks** (full editor) are saved selections: shipped ones (a DMZ, a VoIP closet, a Wi-Fi cell…) or your own. Inserting one moves each of its subnets to the next free one of the same size, so a second copy never conflicts with the first. Each shipped block brings the router or firewall its devices use as their gateway (an edge router also brings its Internet cloud), so it goes in with no Gateway finding; an edge device's own uplink to the rest of your map is left blank for you to fill in.
- **Tidy** lays the map out in tiers (internet, router, switches), then each switch's devices in port order, at most 12 to a row, with each switch above its own devices and each tier above the one it serves. With groups, each group's devices are laid out as a block inside its parent's, and no two blocks overlap. A rack is laid out as one column in unit order, top unit at the top, like the rack view. A VM or container goes beside the host it runs on (on the host's row in a rack, under the host otherwise), whatever it is cabled to. Notes go with the devices they point at, keeping their place beside them. With two or more devices selected it arranges only those. Its ▾ menu has **Tidy all**, which arranges the whole map whatever is selected, and, while a device in a group or a group's devices are selected, **Tidy group**, which arranges that group (the groups inside it and what runs on its hosts included) where it stands, nothing else moving. With several devices selected it is the innermost group they all share.
- Undo and redo work per action: a typed field, an inserted block, a batch of added devices or a Tidy is one step each. Ctrl+Z (Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y to redo) undoes the diagram's last step from the map and from a tick box, a select or a button too; inside a text field it is the field's own text undo.
- While the landing, a dialog, the shortcut list or Add devices is open, the map's keys (F, T, 1 to 3, /, Del, the arrows in the rack view, Ctrl+A and Ctrl+Z) do nothing to the map behind it, and Ctrl+Z undoes nothing at all unless the focus is in one of the overlay's text fields (then it is that field's own undo): Esc closes the topmost one only (a dialog over the landing leaves the landing), and ? closes the shortcut list.
- **Zoom.** The wheel zooms about the pointer; the + and − buttons (and the + and − keys) zoom about the middle of the view, so what is there stays there. The 1:1 button goes back to the start (on a phone it zooms to 1 about the middle of the view instead), and the Fit button (⤢) frames the whole network, as F does.
- **Dark map.** The ◐ button under the zoom buttons turns the map's background dark with the drawing in white: black with the Carino theme, blueprint blue with the Colourful one. Cable colours, findings and the selection keep their colours. It is kept in this browser; the PNG, SVG and report diagram stay on a light background.
- **Motion.** A collapse, an expand, a Tidy, an undo or redo and a drop are played rather than jumped to: devices glide to their new places, fly into a group as it collapses and spread out of it as it opens, a frame eases to its new size and the one a drop went into pulses once. In the rack view a box that is moved or resized slides to its units and the cables fade back in. Only the picture moves; files, links and exports are the same either way. It is off when the system asks for reduced motion.

## Spreadsheets and pasted output

- **Export** (full editor, Export → Spreadsheet): CSV sheets (devices, interfaces, cables, ports, subnets, Wi-Fi once the network has a Wi-Fi network, groups, providers once the network has one, saved searches once the network has one, findings) in UTF-8, or the same rows copied as TSV. The subnets sheet carries each subnet's name, VLAN, gateway and DNS, which devices inherit, and its DHCP pool (`dhcp_first`, `dhcp_last`; "DHCP start" and "DHCP end" headings are read as them too, and an end that is no address is refused on its row), so subnets, devices, interfaces (with bonds), cables and ports (imported after the cables) imported into an empty map give the whole network back; without the ports sheet the ports' labels, VLANs and PoE are lost, and a powered device then shows as on a port that gives no PoE. The devices sheet has `vendor`, `model` and `os` (Firmware / OS) in that order (headings such as "Manufacturer", "Make", "Brand" or "Fabricante" are read as the vendor, "Model", "Model number" or "Modelo" as the model, and "Firmware", "Firmware version" or the old "Model / firmware" as the firmware; two rows of one name that disagree on the vendor or the model stay two devices; a network that already had a column of its own called "Vendor" keeps it, its devices.csv has both the `vendor` column and that one, and each reads back into its own), `group` (the group's path), `rack_u`, `rack_height`, `rack_face`, `rack_depth` (`full` or `half` for a device in a rack), `ports_face` (`front` or `rear`, the device's own or its type's; one that says its type's is not stored) `poe_budget` and `poe_draw` (watts; a draw may be given as `class 0` to `class 8`, held as that class's watts; a budget on anything but a switch or access point, or a draw on a cloud, VM or container, is said on the row and not kept) and `runs_on` (a VM's or a container's host by its name, or by its id when another device has the name; on import a host further down the same sheet is found too, and a host it may not run on is said on the row and left out) columns; a depth column headed "Half depth" or "Shallow" reads a yes as half depth, any other depth column a yes as full; on import, groups the sheet names that the map does not have are made only after you tick the box for them. The groups sheet holds each group's id, name, kind, parent and a rack's height, width, depth in mm and numbering (`bottom` or `top`); a sheet that makes a rack shorter says in the review how many placed devices lose their units. The cables sheet carries everything the rack view's patch schedule shows (both ends' devices and ports, and the cable's label, colour, length and via): a switch or access point port left blank on a new cable's row is read as no port there (the rack view's no-port spot), not one picked for it, so a cable nobody patched yet comes back unpatched, while a blank port on a device's own NIC is still its NIC; and the devices sheet's `switch` and `port` columns leave out a cable with no port at the switch, which the cables sheet carries instead. The Wi-Fi sheet holds each network's SSID, security, bands, subnet (by its CIDR) and hidden flag; the interfaces sheet carries a wired interface's `untagged_vlan` and `tagged_vlans` (blank on a wireless one; "Native VLAN" and "Tagged VLANs" headings are read as them, a bare "VLAN" heading is left unmapped), and each radio's standard, bands and, per band, channel, width, power and the SSIDs it broadcasts (`ch_2g`, `width_5g`, `ssids_6g`…; an SSID is written by name, or by its `wifi_id` when the name would read back as something else: shared by two networks, holding a `;`, or reading `none`); the cables sheet carries each wireless link's `ssid`, `band` and `signal`. Import the subnets and Wi-Fi sheets before the sheets that name their networks. The saved searches sheet holds each search's `search_id`, name, `query` (exactly as typed, never translated), `columns` (Topo's column keys and the network's own columns by name, separated by `; `; blank is as the Devices table shows them; a column of your own whose name holds a `;` or would read back as one of Topo's goes by its `x:<id>` key), `sort` (a column the same way; blank is as listed), `descending` and `in_report` (yes or no). On import a row is found by its id, then by its name; a column or sort may also be a Devices table heading in English or the page's language. A sheet of only these headings with a `query` and a `name` column is read as saved searches even without `search_id`. Names are checked against what the whole sheet leaves, so two searches can swap names and a name one row renames away is free for another. A new row needs a name and a search; a search Topo does not understand or one that reads what this browser alone remembers (`was:`, `has:history`), a column that names nothing (an `x:<id>` key the network has no column for included), a name another saved search still has, a second row on one search (by id, a new one's included) or one name, more than 20 columns and more than 30 searches are refused on their row, while a row that leaves a search's text as it is keeps it. A cell that would start a formula (`=`, `+`, `-`, `@`) goes out with an apostrophe in front and comes back without it. A sheet exported and imported back changes nothing.
- **Import** takes a CSV file or rows pasted from a spreadsheet. Columns are matched by heading (`Hostname`, `IP Address`, `MAC Address`, `Switch`, `Port`…) or, without headings, by what the values look like, and you can change any match. Rows are matched to devices by id, then asset tag, then MAC, then IP, then name (a name two devices share is a conflict, not a guess). A row found only by its address or name that names another device (another asset tag or serial, or two of another MAC, name and type) is a conflict, not an edit of the device that holds that address. Only mapped columns are applied and a blank cell changes nothing. The review lists the first 500 rows of a longer sheet and says how many more there are, one or many; its counts cover every row, listed or not, and Apply writes every one that is not a conflict. A row that matches nothing adds a device, cabled to the switch and port it names. A sheet of ports sets labels, access VLANs, tagged VLANs (`port_tagged`, written in order with ranges, `10,20,30-39`, or `all`) and PoE (`port_poe`: `af`, `at`, `bt3`, `bt4` or watts; 802.3af, PoE, PoE+, PoE++ and Type 3 or 4 are read too) on ports already drawn and never cables anything; a list with a VLAN outside 1-4094 is refused whole on its row (`10 - 20`, spaced, is a range), and a bare "PoE" heading is not a port's (an inventory's PoE column leaves it a sheet of devices). A switch's own port list headed "Native VLAN" and "Tagged VLANs" (or "Trunk VLANs", "Allowed VLANs") beside its device and port columns, and nothing of an interface's, is a sheet of ports too; on a sheet of devices those headings set a wired interface's VLANs, and a wireless interface's VLAN cells are passed over with a note. A device's PoE draw is read only from a heading that says PoE (`poe_draw`, "PoE draw"): an inventory's "Power draw" column (mains power) is left unmatched. A sheet of subnets (a `cidr` or `network_id` column) declares or edits subnets; it never moves one to another range, which is Renumber's job.
- **Paste anything**: the same box reads an ARP table or a DHCP lease list (Linux, macOS, Windows, Cisco, HP/Aruba, Juniper, MikroTik, dnsmasq, ISC dhcpd, Fortinet…) and takes each address with its MAC and, when given, a name. It also reads network scans: nmap's normal output and its XML (`-oX`), where a host found up without a MAC (on another network, or the scanning machine) is still taken by its address, and arp-scan and netdiscover, whose maker column is never read as a name. Advanced IP Scanner and Angry IP Scanner CSV exports map as ordinary sheets. A name seen on the network never renames a device you named, and is never used to match one, because DHCP names repeat.
- **Excel files** from Windows are read in the code page Excel saved them in when they are not UTF-8 (Windows-1252, or 1251 with the page in Russian and Shift_JIS in Japanese), and the review says so. Dates like `03/04/2019` are read day-first or month-first per column, from any value in it past 12; a column that never says is read the page language's way, and the review says which. Rows that name the same new device (a sheet with a row per NIC) are one device, unless they disagree on its serial, asset tag, type, model, location or group, or the MAC is randomised.
- Every import goes through a review that lists each row as added, changed, unchanged or a conflict, with what the checks would find afterwards. Apply is one undo step.

## Imports from other tools

- **Import…** (full editor, Export → Import…; also what Open a file and a drop on the landing do with a file that is not Topo's): pick one or several files (an export is often a set; a zip is opened). Each file says what it was read as, with **Not right?** to pick another format or leave it out. A Topo .json is offered to open as a network and a Topo spreadsheet to open in Spreadsheet, so they keep their own doors. A UniFi controller backup (.unf) or an Omada one (.cfg) is a locked file and is refused with a sentence saying what to export instead. **Read the files** shows **What was understood**: counts of devices, interfaces, cables, subnets, VLANs, groups, racks, Wi-Fi networks, providers and custom columns; what could not be read, by file and row (the first 50, then "and N more"); notes ("VLAN 30 has no subnet: it stays on its ports", "2 passwords and keys were left out", records of types it does not read). When the files hold passing devices, **Include** has a tick for each kind with its count: **Clients (wired)** ticked, **Clients (wireless)** and **DHCP leases** not (D121); the counts follow the ticks. A Cisco capture with no prompt asks for **This switch's name**. Then where it goes: **Merge into** the open network (the default when it is kept), **Merge into another network…**, or **Open as its own network**. **Continue** opens the usual merge review, two-way, as a copy of a network: a restore point first ("Before importing …"), a log entry naming the files, one undo step. Importing adds and updates; it never deletes. Passwords, keys and SNMP communities are never read. Records keep the source's own ids (`nb-<source>-d12`), so importing the same export again updates instead of duplicating; a record whose source has no key is matched by asset tag, serial, MAC, address or name.
- **NetBox**: the REST API's JSON saved to files (`/api/dcim/devices/?limit=0` and the like; a page, a list, or several pages in one file), a list view's CSV export, or bulk-import CSV. Sites (when several) and locations become groups, racks rack groups, devices and virtual machines devices (role to type, make, model, platform, serial, asset tag, rack position and face, status), a switch's or AP's numbered ports its port grid (labels, VLANs, PoE), LAGs bonds, addresses go on their interfaces (one on no interface is listed and skipped), prefixes become subnets named by their VLANs, cables and wireless links cables, wireless LANs Wi-Fi networks, providers providers; tags become a "Tags" column and custom fields custom columns. NetBox 4.2's separate MAC addresses are read.
- **nmap** (`nmap -oX`): the hosts that were up become devices with their address (in the subnet the command line names, else /32 with a note), MAC and maker (from the scan, else Topo's own maker list), host name (a reverse-DNS name loses its domain), the open ports and services as a notes line ("Open: 22/tcp ssh (OpenSSH 9.6p1), 443/tcp https"), and the OS when nmap is 85% sure or more (else "OS guess: … (70%)" in the notes). Nothing goes in Allowed Ports. The type comes from what answered (9100 or IPP a printer, RTSP a camera, SIP a phone, Windows a PC, Linux with SSH a server, router systems a router). Hosts that were down are counted; several scans picked together are one set, a device seen in two of them is one device.
- **UniFi**: what its controller API gives, saved to files (`stat/device`, `stat/sta`, `rest/networkconf`, `rest/wlanconf`; one list per file or several in one), and the Network application's client and device lists as CSV. Gateways are routers; switches and access points get their port grid (a port's name that is not "Port N" is its label, PoE, the native VLAN); each uplink is a cable to the parent's port, a mesh AP a wireless link. Clients are wired to their switch port or associated with their AP on their SSID, with band and signal. Networks become subnets (gateway, VLAN, DHCP and its range; the WAN is not one) and Wi-Fi networks keep their security and whether they are hidden; passphrases are never read. Records keep UniFi's ids, so importing again updates them.
- **Omada**: its Open API's JSON saved to files (devices, clients, LAN and wireless networks) and the controller's device and client lists as CSV, read the same way as UniFi. The field names are from knowledge of Omada, not checked against a live controller.
- **MikroTik** (`/export`, RouterOS 6 or 7, terse or verbose): one export is one device (its identity, model, RouterOS version, serial) with its interfaces (renamed ports, bridges, VLAN interfaces carried tagged by their parent, bonds, wireless and wifi), addresses, DHCP networks with gateway, DNS and pool, Wi-Fi networks with security and bands, and DHCP leases as clients (not cabled). A CRS or CSS switch's etherN ports are its port grid, with each bridge port's PVID as its VLAN and the bridge VLAN table's tagged lists (ranges written out). The output of `/ip neighbor print detail` pasted at the end adds the neighbours, cabled from the port they were seen on. Sections it does not read are named; secrets are never read.
- **Cisco** (`show cdp neighbors detail`, `show mac address-table`; IOS, IOS-XE, NX-OS): the switch (named by its prompt, or asked), its neighbours (type from their capabilities, model from the platform, the serial NX-OS shows) and a cable between the two ports, with the native VLAN. From the MAC table, each address on an access port becomes a device named after its maker ("Dell 0003"), cabled to the port with the port's VLAN, so the merge finds the devices already in the network by their MAC. A port with more than 3 addresses, a port-channel or a switch, router or AP behind it is an uplink and its addresses are left out; a phone with a PC behind it makes the port carry the second VLAN tagged. Both are said in a note (D119). Several prompts in one file are several switches.
- **Lansweeper** (asset CSV, Classic or Sites): each asset is a device with its name, type (from the asset type and OS), address, MAC, maker, model, serial (placeholders such as "To be filled by O.E.M." dropped), OS and version, description as notes, Building and Location as groups (or the location as text), state (anything but Active also in a "Lansweeper state" column), purchase date as In service since, and Warranty, Last seen and Department columns. Dates written 31/12/2026 or 12/31/2026 are read once the column shows which; a column that never does is left empty and said (D124). AssetID keeps the device the same on the next import.
- **phpIPAM** (CSV exports of subnets, addresses and VLANs, or the API's JSON saved to files): subnets with their VLAN and description, addresses as devices (host name, else the address) with MAC, notes and owner, in their subnet's prefix; a gateway address is its subnet's gateway; Device and Port cable it to that switch's port. Offline is retired, Reserved planned, Used and DHCP active. A VLAN with no subnet is said.
- **Export to NetBox…** (Export menu): asks NetBox 4.2–4.4 or 4.1, and downloads `topo_<org>_<site>_<date>_netbox.zip`: NetBox's bulk-import CSV files numbered in import order and a README.txt with the order and what is not carried. What NetBox has no field for rides in `topo_*` custom fields (`topo_more` keeps each record exactly), so importing the zip back into Topo gives the same network, while edits made in NetBox to names, serials, asset tags, comments, platforms, makes, models, MACs and cable labels are read back. Names NetBox needs unique are made unique ("AP (2)"), a second cable on a port gets a generated interface, and VPN links and links to virtual machines stay in the device's `topo_more` (the README lists them). Not carried: map notes, the report header, saved searches, the table layout, accepted findings and pins, problem reports, the log. The column lists follow NetBox's documented import forms and were not checked against a live NetBox.

## Reports, labels and exports

- **Site report**: a printable document with a header you fill in (site, client, author, reference, date, scope; a blank site is the name the network is kept under), the findings with accepted ones listed separately with their reasons, an inventory with one line per interface (a powered device's line saying "PoE 6.5 W") and Model (the vendor and model together) and Firmware / OS columns (a host's VMs and containers listed under it, stepped in), the inventory under a heading per group when there are groups, an **Inventory** section after it when any device has a vendor, a model or a firmware / OS (the Table's Inventory tab on paper: **By vendor**, **By model** with each model's firmware versions and counts, a mismatch marked as its firmware finding is or said accepted, and **By firmware / OS**, each with the devices in service or planned and the retired ones in a muted column; plain text, nothing in it that reaches outside the page; a vendor the MAC only suggests is not printed; a binder prints each site's own), an elevation of each rack (front and rear, numbered as its rails are, what runs on each host beside it and listed under the rack, as wide as the rack against a 19″ one, under its size, each device with a small strip of its ports on the face they are on, free hollow and cabled filled, then the rack's patch schedule, a row per cable in the rack's order with both ends, the tag or "no tag", the colour, the length and the via), a port map for every switch and access point, cabled or not yet (with labels and VLANs when the ports have them, Tagged and PoE columns on a switch whose ports have them (the VLAN column then reads Untagged), "PoE: 47 of 120 W budget" in the heading of a switch with a budget, free ports as ranges, and "Almost full" or "No free port" beside one the port-capacity note names; one whose port count was never set says "port count not set" after its total, which is a guess, and lists no free ports; a bond with no cable on it still takes its ports, and the map says so; a one-port access point or a retired box with nothing on it is left out), a Wi-Fi section when the network has anything wireless (see Wi-Fi below), a **VLANs** section after Addressing once any port or wired interface records a tagged VLAN (one row per VLAN the register names: its subnet and gateway, how many ports have it untagged with no tagged list, and the trunks that carry it tagged, by device and port, or one All VLANs row when the only trunks carry every VLAN and no VLAN is named; what is written only, nothing checked), a **Capacity** section after Addressing when there is any subnet, a PoE budget, or (with Capacity: ports table ticked) a switch or access point nearly out of free ports (an Addresses table of every subnet: its size, how many addresses are in use and how many are free, the nearly full ones marked, an undeclared one muted, an IPv6 one as 2^64 with its use only; when any subnet records a DHCP pool, three more columns: the pool, **In pool** (its addresses recorded here of its size) and **Fixed free**, with a pool that does not count marked "not counted"; then, only when **Capacity: ports table** is ticked (off by default, since the port map above already lists every switch's ports), a Ports table of the nearly full switches: the device, its ports, how many are in use, and the free ones; then a PoE table of every switch or access point with a PoE budget: its budget, what the devices on its PoE ports draw and what is left, marked when under nothing), a **Saved searches** section after Capacity for the network's saved searches ticked for this report (see **Search**: a heading per search, the search as typed in monospace with "Devices: n of total", then a table of the matching devices with Device first and the search's columns in its order, sorted as it says or else in the inventory's order with a VM under its host; every address in full, a type in words, a yes/no column and NAT as Yes or No, and ages and services counted to the report's date, so `has:finding` finds what the Findings section counts: open findings, as of that date; a search that matches nothing says "No devices match.", one Topo does not understand says so in a muted line, and one that reads what this browser alone remembers, `was:` or `has:history`, is not printed and says why, so the report is the same whoever prints it), what changed since the last back-up, an **Address history** section after it only when its tick is on (off by default, and only for a network kept in this browser that has a history: see **History**; a device per run of rows, each address, MAC, switch port, group or name that changed since the previous report, or since the first back-up or report recorded when there has been none, with the value now and "since" its first point, or "now" when typed since, then each past value with its dates and back-up, report or file (a value typed back to what was last recorded reads as now, once); a removed device last, with the date it went, or "removed after" the last back-up or report when it was deleted since; a NIC no longer there reads "removed interface" (numbered when there are several, and on a removed device each NIC reads "interface 1", "interface 2"), with the address of a NIC that replaced it beside it; a device that never changed is left out, and with no change at all the section says nothing has changed since then; plain text, nothing in it that reaches outside the page; the Simple summary never prints it; a binder prints each site's own history on that site's sheet), and the diagram. The diagram goes where its labels can still be read: in the text while a device's name prints at a readable size there; and otherwise on one landscape page of its own at the end, filled edge to edge, however large the network (the lettered grid of sheets is the Large map's, below). The picture is carried once however many pages show it, and a very large network's picture is drawn with fewer pixels so the browser can still make it. The pages turn on their side without fixing a paper size, so A4 and Carta both print. Everything in it that depends on the date (ages, services due, schedules, "in N days" and "ago", and so the counts, the verdict and the diagram's folded groups) counts to the report's date, or to today when the date is blank, so a report made again later says what it said. Two ticks say what goes in, both on whenever the dialog opens: **Simple summary**, one page in plain words (a verdict at the top: looks fine, things that need attention, or problems to fix; each open problem in everyday words with why it matters and the devices it is about, faults first; the devices counted by kind and listed with where they are and the network's own columns the table shows, or the kinds only past 60 devices; **Room to grow**, when a switch's or access point's port count is set or a subnet can run out: how many free ports the switches, the access points, or both have (named for what the site has), and the ones almost or quite full by name (six at most, then "…and n more"); then how many free addresses the networks have (IPv4 subnets of 8 addresses or more, each free address counted once when one network lies inside another, and a subnet only retired devices use left out), and the ones almost or quite full by name, cut the same way (one with a DHCP pool by its fixed addresses left outside the automatic range); what changed since the last visit; who checked it), then the map on a landscape page of its own, and **Technical details**, the document above. With both, the summary comes first; one always stays ticked. Under **Technical details**, **Sections** has a tick per part of the technical sheet, in print order: Findings (with To check on the next visit and Known / accepted), Providers, Topology (the diagram, also its landscape page; the Large map is its own tick), Device inventory, Inventory, Racks, Switch ports, Connections, Wi-Fi, Addressing, VLANs, Capacity: addresses, Capacity: ports table, Capacity: PoE budgets, Saved searches, Changes since the last back-up or report, Network log, and Address history. Every one starts ticked whenever the dialog opens except **Capacity: ports table**, **Network log** (the network's merges, see **Keeping your work**) and **Address history**, so a report nobody unticks prints what it always did; nothing about them is remembered, in Settings or the network. Unticking one leaves just that section out, its heading too (a Capacity with none of its three tables ticked has no heading); the summary at the top of the sheet (the device, connection, subnet and finding counts) and Scope and method always print, and the Simple summary is not changed by any of them. A section with nothing to show for this network is left out as before, ticked or not. The binder dialog has the same ticks, applied to every site's sheet, and Findings, Addressing and Topology also leave out the cover's Findings across the estate, Estate addressing and Estate topology. With Topology unticked and Large map ticked, a Topology heading still says the large map is at the end. With Switch ports unticked, the Capacity Ports table no longer points at it. A report handed over with neither the Simple summary nor the Changes section does not count as the last report: the next one still lists the changes since the last report that showed them. One handed over with **Address history** ticked still notes a report in the history (and leaves the Changes baseline where it was), so the next report's Address history counts from it. While the network has saved searches, and while the Saved searches section is ticked, **Saved searches** has a tick per search, each starting from its print mark whenever the dialog opens; ticking one changes only this report and is never stored, so it works on a read-only network too. The summary never prints them, and a binder prints each site's marked ones on that site's sheet. A third tick, **Large map** (off by default, with the technical details), adds the diagram again at the end on a grid of landscape sheets you pick, **Sheets across** × **Sheets down** (1 to 6 each, 2 × 2 to start), to read up close or tape together: an overview with each square lettered (A1, B1…), then one sheet per square, each with a small map of the grid in its corner and the squares it continues on. Under the pickers, **Sheets to print** has a square per sheet, all on to start: tapping one leaves its sheet out (and tapping it again puts it back), and so does clicking its square on the preview's overview. A square left out stays on the overview, faded, with no sheet of its own and no "continues on" pointing at it; the total says how many of the grid print ("= 4 of 6 sheets"), and the last one printed cannot be left out. The squares left out are kept while the grid changes size and cleared each time the dialog opens. After any change to the large map (the tick, the grid size, a square) the preview opens at its overview, so the grid is in view; any other edit redraws the preview where it was scrolled to. The grid is even (every two neighbours overlap by the same small strip), each sheet the shape of the page, and centred on the network; nothing about it is worked out for you. Unticked, there is no grid and no sheet marks anywhere. The dialog shows the report beside its questions, the pages as they will print, redrawn a moment after each edit (on a phone under them, scaled to whole pages): **Print** prints it (or saves it as PDF) and keeps the dialog open for a copy, **Download** saves it as an .html file and closes the dialog, and **Close** hands nothing over. The header typed in is stored when the report is printed or downloaded. A kept network's "changes since the last report" counts from the last report that was printed or downloaded, not one that was only previewed. The binder report does the same per site: with both, each site's summary before its technical sheet; the summary alone is a page per site with no estate cover and no diagrams to draw.
- **Print where they are plugged in**: the Locate sheet for a selection of devices (see Locate above).
- **Print labels**: 62 × 29 mm labels for a selection. Each tagged device gets its tag, name, model (vendor and model, or its firmware / OS when neither is recorded), address and MAC with a QR code of its asset link, drawn in the page; each labelled cable gets two, one per end. It opens a print preview first, each label on a page of its own as it prints (the status counts them): **Print** prints them, **Download** saves the sheet (`topo_<organization>_<network>_<date>_labels.html`), and **Close**, Esc or a click outside saves nothing.
- **Asset links**: `topo.carino.systems/#asset/<tag>` opens the network in this browser that holds that asset tag and selects the device.
- **Images**: PNG, WEBP, and SVG (full editor) with the site's name as its title and nothing in it that reaches outside the file.
- **Netplan**: on a PC, server, VM or container, **Open in Netplan ↗** opens [Netplan](https://netplan.carino.systems/) filled in with that device's interfaces.
- **File names**: every download of a network is `topo_<organization>_<site>_<YYYY-MM-DD>`, then what it is: `topo_acme_casa-roma_2026-11-09.json` (the back-up), `.svg` and `.png` (the map), `topo_acme_casa-roma_2026-11-09_report.html` (and `_summary`, `_labels`, `_locate`), `topo_acme_casa-roma_2026-11-09_devices.csv` (one per sheet). The organization is the report header's **Client**; the site is the network's card name, or the report's site when it is not kept, the same for every download of it (a site report too: its header's Site heads the page, but the file says the card's name); a report is dated by its own Date when that is a YYYY-MM-DD day, and by today otherwise. Each part is lowercased, accents folded, any run of other characters a hyphen (letters of any script are kept), so `_` only ever separates parts; an empty part is left out, a site that repeats the client is said once, client and site together are kept within 180 bytes (the longer trimmed first, so a long Japanese or Russian name never pushes the date and kind past a file name's 255-byte limit), and with neither the name is `topo_network_<date>`. A binder (Back up all) is `topo_<organization>_<date>.json` when its networks name one client (a network with no client typed, and templates and blocks, do not count against it), or `topo_networks_<date>.json` when they name several or none; an estate report naming no site or client is `topo_networks_<date>_report.html`. A restore point's download is named the same way, dated by the point. A binder bound to a file (Save to binder) keeps the name it was given; the picker only suggests the binder's name. A file named this way and carrying no name of its own opens as a card named after its site part (a browser's ` (1)` on a repeated download is ignored). Older files (`clinica-norte-2026-10-26.json`, `networks-2026-10-26.json`, `.nettopo`) still open.
- **File format**: a back-up or binder is JSON that says what it is. Its first key is `"$schema": "https://topo.carino.systems/schema/topo-v19.json"` (the number is the document version, `DOC_VERSION`, or a newer document's own version when a read-only file from a newer Topo is backed up as it came), then the envelope it always had: `kind` (`nettopology` for one network, `nettopo-binder` for a binder), `version` (the envelope's, 1), then `name` and `doc`, or `exported` and `sites`. It is saved as `application/vnd.carino.topo+json` with a `.json` extension, and Open a file accepts that type beside `.json` and `.nettopo`. Nothing fetches the address and readers ignore the key, so older Topo builds and the 0.0.1 desktop apps open these files as before, and a file without it still opens. The schema itself is `schema/topo-v19.json` in the repository (JSON Schema 2020-12; `topo-v18.json`, `topo-v17.json` and `topo-v16.json` stay beside it for files written before), field by field since step 6 (docs/SCOPE.md D120): every key of every record, its values and limits, and `schema/topo-backup-v1.json` for a server backup. The written description is **docs/FORMAT.md** (the envelopes, every field, ids, providers, problem reports, the log's hash chain, revisions, the merge rules, the migrations and rules for other tools), linked from the About tab; every sample, block, migrated old document and import result is checked against the schema (tests/suites/formatschema.js). Document version 17 adds, beside `id` and `updated`: `rev`, a token naming this copy as it left (a new one only when the network changed since the last copy that left), `base`, the copy it was started from, and `prior`, the up to 8 tokens it carried before, newest first (a binder carries the three on each site, before its `doc`); and, last in the document, `log`, the network log (see **Keeping your work**). `rev`, `base` and `prior` are identity, not content: they are written only where a copy leaves (a back-up, a binder, a link, a restore download), never by an edit, and are not an undo step or a change. None of the four is a sheet: the CSV export and import are unchanged, and the round trip stays lossless. Document version 18 makes a device's, a cable's and a group's provider a list (`providers`, ids in the order of `doc.providers`; a v17 `provider` is lifted into a list of one, and an item naming none writes no key) and adds `incidents`, the problem reports (an id, when, a status that only moves forward, the symptom, the devices or group, the client's words, the reporter's name and phone or email, the providers it went to, when it was sent, its visits, when and why it was closed), before `log`; the log gains the kinds `incident` (opened, sent, closed, reopened) and `visit` (who came for which provider, when, the outcome, notes and what changed since the copy they received) in the same hash chain. The reports are not a sheet either. A merge matches a report by its id, takes the later status without asking (a reopen counts as a round up, so an older closed copy cannot undo it), unions its devices, providers and visits, and never removes one: only the cap of 200 does, oldest closed first. After the bump, the first copy of a kept network that leaves gets a new `rev` once, because the content hash includes the version. Document version 19 (step 7) adds the log's `decision` kind (an owner's answer on a provider's visit: applied or rejected, and whether the visit succeeded), a device's answer checks (`checks`, at most 8: the protocol, port and options, the interval, "slow above" and which monitors ask it; never a password) and availability `target`, a provider's `target` and `targetHours`, outages as incidents (`kind: "outage"`, with the times down, the devices behind it, the monitor and the acts on it) with their own `incident` log events, and a visit's `monitoring` section. States, samples and statistics are never in the document. A v18 document migrates with nothing changed but the version.

Safari and Firefox: no section, downloads as before.

When this device now holds an address or MAC another device on the map held before, a line says whose it was ("Was CAM-03's until 2026-09-14"; an address is compared without its prefix, so 192.168.1.20/24 and 192.168.1.20/16 are the same address); it is a hint only, never a finding, and not said when both hold it now (that is a duplicate finding). **Forget this device's history** asks first and forgets that device's past only; **Clear history**, among a card's tools on the landing page once the card has a history, asks first and clears the whole card's. With nothing recorded yet the section says Topo notes these values at each back-up and report. History is kept in this browser only, and the section says so: it does not travel with files, links or binders (it is printed only in a report whose **Address history** tick you turn on; see **Site report**), so a network opened on another computer, or from a back-up, starts with none. **Older back-ups can be read in**: opening a back-up file of a network kept here whose card is newer offers **Add to its history** beside Open as a copy and Open mine, and a binder holding older copies of networks kept here asks once, after its merge, whether to add them to their history (Not now adds nothing). What the file held goes into the history at the time the file was saved, in its place among the back-ups and reports already recorded (a value held either side and at that time stays one span; one that differed splits it; a device only the file holds is a removed one), marked "file"; the card, the network on screen and the undo history are left as they are, and a toast says it was added. A file of any Topo version this build opens is read (one from a newer Topo, or one with no saved time, is not offered); the same file read twice adds nothing, nor does a back-up whose own back-up or report is already recorded with the same values (its point was noted when it went out, just after the edit the file is dated by), and a binder opened again does not ask about copies already in the history; templates and blocks take no history this way (neither door offers it); one older than the 200 points of history kept is too old and says so; with recording turned off the choice is not offered. A network that is not kept here, or is read-only, shows that history is kept only for networks kept in this browser. Edits between back-ups and reports are not recorded. The simple views do not show it unless Settings' **Show address history in Simple view** is ticked (off by default; the owner's call, 2026-11-05): then, under the device's Locate sentence, a few plain read-only sentences say what it held before, newest first, one per past value ("Was 192.168.1.20 until 2026-10-03.", "Was plugged into Office switch, socket 12 until …", or "Was on Wi-Fi through Lobby AP until …" for a past radio link, in the Locate sentence's words, "Was in Building A / Rack 3 until …", "Was called PC-1 until …"), four at most and then "Earlier changes are in the Full editor."; as they name no NIC, an address the device still holds on another NIC (a replacement NIC, a bond, two NICs swapped) is not listed as a past one, and an address it had on two NICs is one line; no MAC, no buttons, no Forget, nothing that changes the network or makes an undo step. Unticked, the Simple views are exactly as before.

- **Revisions**: every copy of a network that leaves (Back up, Back up all and the bound binder file, a share link, a binder link, a restore point's download) carries a revision token, and the copy's content is kept under it in this browser, beside the card, so that when the copy comes back edited Topo can find what it started from and tell your edits from theirs (the merge, docs/SCOPE.md step 2). The token changes only when the network did: backing up twice in a row, or after an edit that was undone, gives the same one. A file, binder site or link you keep becomes a card that is that revision and was started from it; **Keep as a copy** starts a new network from it. Counting or previewing a binder stamps nothing. - **Network log** (`log` in the file, document v17): an append-only list of what was done to the network from outside, kept in the network so it travels with it: each merge, with where it came from (a file's name, a link or a binder), when, who (Settings' **Prepared by** name, when one is set), how many devices and other parts were added, changed and removed and how many conflicts there were. On a phone the rows stack and every button is full width and 44px tall. Translated (es, pt-BR, ja, ru): field names are the table's column labels; values are shown as typed.

A network made by a newer Topo (read-only), a copy made by one, a template or a block cannot be merged.
- **Restore points**: before a file, a binder (opened, or read from the bound binder file at Back up all), a link kept over a network you have, a merge, or a spreadsheet applied to a kept network changes it, Topo keeps a copy of the network as it was. - **Merging a copy** (js/mergedoors.js): no door replaces a network with a newer copy unasked any more; a copy that comes back is merged.

A file made by a newer Topo opens read-only and is never merged.
  - **Finding the copy both started from**: of the revisions the copy names (its `prior` revisions, its `base`, the revisions its log merged in), the ones this network holds and grew from (one it emitted or arrived as, or one it merged and still names in its log), and of those the nearest: the one held here last. A link visited and edited before **Keep** is merged with its edits, but the revision it arrived with is kept as it arrived. A copy whose own `rev` is yours but whose content was edited after it left (by hand) is compared with that rev. A copy from before revisions (no `rev`, no `base`) edited after your last back-up is compared with that back-up ("It is compared with your back-up of …"); one older than the back-up is merged two-way.
  - **The card's ⇄ Merge…** (network cards only): **A file into this network…** (a file picker) or **This network into another…** (the same list, without this one); the network merged from stays as it is and leaves with a revision, so a later copy of it finds this merge as its base. ## Reporting a problem

- **Report a problem** (docs/SCOPE.md step 4) is for the client, who is not technical: a button in the Simple and imagenology views (in the device list, beside the empty panel and on a phone's handy bar as ⚠) and **⚠ Report a problem…** in the Export menu, translated throughout. A read-only network (from a newer Topo) refuses it. One sheet, top to bottom, phone first (every control at least 44px on touch screens, nearly full height at 640px and under):
  - **What is wrong**: seven tiles, No internet, Wi-Fi, Phones, Cameras, Printer, Power cut, Something else.
  - **Where**: the device selected when the sheet opened, **Tap it on the map** (the sheet steps aside and a banner asks for the device, with Cancel), **Choose from a list…** (the devices of the likely kind first: cameras and NVRs for a camera problem, printers for a printer, and so on), and the place (**Whole place** or a group); several devices are chips with ✕.
  - **What happened**: up to 2000 characters, counted.
  - **Your name and a phone or an email**: needed, and remembered in this browser (Settings, **Problem reports**) unless you untick **Remember in this browser**. They are never put in a URL.
  - **Whom to contact**, worked out as you choose: the providers ranked for the problem, each with why ("Internet for Internet, 2 steps upstream", "IT support for Office (its group)"), Open now or Closed now when its hours say, a call button (`tel:`) and an email button, and a **Send to** tick (the first one ticked, and any tied with it). With no provider at all it says so and suggests adding one in Providers; the report can still be saved and sent to anyone.
- **Routing** (js/routing.js, pure and offline): a symptom names the services to look for, in order (No internet: Internet, then IT support; Wi-Fi: IT support, then Internet; Phones: Phone / VoIP, then IT support; Cameras: CCTV / alarm, then IT support; Printer: IT support, then Hardware vendor; Power cut: Power / UPS; Something else: IT support, then anyone). From each device, Topo walks the cables to the network's edges (each internet cloud that does not stand for another site; with none, the routers and firewalls), the shortest way to each, so a site with two lines lists both; a VM or container starts at its host. The providers of every device and cable on the way count (with what each device inherits from its groups and hosts), then, for a power cut, whoever looks after Power / UPS in the same place (Topo has no UPS device kind), then the place's groups, then every other provider the network lists ("listed for this network"), so an ISP that nobody tied to a device is still offered. Order: the service's place in the symptom's list (a provider of another service comes after, never hidden), then how near (steps upstream), then open now before unknown before closed, then the Providers tab's order. Support hours are read in simple forms in each language ("24/7", "Mon–Fri 9–18", "L-V 9:00-18:00", "Seg-Sex 8h-17h", "Пн–Пт 9–18", "月〜金 9時-18時", "Every day 7–23", several ranges with commas), in this browser's local time (the file has no time zone); hours that do not read are unknown, never a reason to leave a provider out. Only the shortest paths are walked, so a backup circuit that is longer does not rank level with the main one.
- **Save** keeps the report in the network (status Reported) and **Save and send** goes on to sending it; either is one undo step with an entry in the network log. A network not kept in this browser is kept first, so a visit sent back has something to merge into; a link's copy of a network you already keep is not claimed (Keep this network asks about the merge), and the toast says so.
- **Send** marks the report Sent (when it was first sent, and whom to; Send again adds to the list) and makes a locked link of the network with its reports, with a new passphrase, masked until **Show**, and **Copy passphrase**. The password-in-notes warning runs first (it reads the client's words and visit notes too); with a hit the link waits for **Send anyway**. Then: **Email** copies the link as a hyperlink, "Follow the infrastructure link", and opens the mail program (a `mailto:` to the chosen providers that have an email, subject "Network problem at {site}: {symptom}", body with the client's words, where, who and how to reach them, an "Infrastructure link:" line with an empty line under it to paste into, and a line saying the passphrase comes separately); the step then says "Your mail program is open and the infrastructure link is copied. Paste it under "Infrastructure link:" (Ctrl+V, or on a phone press and hold, then Paste)." A `mailto:` carries plain text only, so the hyperlink travels by the clipboard (rich HTML, the plain URL beside it); the copy runs on every press (after **Copy passphrase** too), so the client only ever sends the passphrase by hand, and the step says "Link copied: paste it in the email under "Infrastructure link:" (Ctrl+V, or on a phone press and hold, then Paste). The passphrase goes another way, such as a call or a text." If the copy fails, the link is shown and selected and that press stops; the next press tries the copy again and opens the mail program whatever happens (checked in Chromium and Firefox). Words that would make the `mailto:` pass 1900 characters are shortened in the email only. **Share…** (the system's share sheet, where the browser has one: plain text with the raw link), **Copy message** (rich: the hyperlink, the reporter's phone and email as links; plain: the raw link), **Download the file**, and a call button per provider. A network too big for a link (over 16,000 characters; the samples measure about 1,000 to 4,400), or a page that cannot lock one (http on a LAN, `file://`), sends the file `topo_<organization>_<site>_<date>_incident.json` to attach instead: an unlocked link never carries a report. If the link cannot be made, the Sent step is undone.
- **What the link carries** (docs/SCOPE.md D91-D95): the send step asks, every time, **Only what {names} look after** (the default, with the counts: "3 devices of theirs, 5 linked devices around them") or **The whole network**. Their part is everything they answer for (named on it, through its host, or through a group: a group naming them brings all its members, nested groups included), in full; the devices up to two cable hops around it (a VPN tunnel is not a hop), the hosts they run on and the devices the report names, with only their name, type, place, the ports facing the provider's side and their addresses (never notes, serials or other providers); the cables among them; the groups to place them; only the chosen providers' own records (not the client's notes about them); this report, their other reports on their items and their own visits; the site and the client. Changing the choice makes a new link (no second Sent) and checks for passwords in notes again; a file sent instead carries the same part. When the provider's copy comes back, the merge reads what the part left out as unchanged, never as removed: a linked device's hidden fields stay the client's, a linked device they removed is asked and kept by default, and something the client deleted since does not come back. Every part sent stays in this browser until a copy of it is merged back or its report is marked fixed, however many are out (over the store's space, parts sent for no report and those from before 0.0.2 go first; with only parts for open reports left, the library says "Old sent copies are taking up space: close or merge them."); something another provider's copy added meanwhile is the same as a new one of theirs only by its id, serial or MAC: by name, address, asset tag or place it is a question, **Add as new** by default, so two providers' "AP-9"s stay two (D152, D176-D177, D179).
- **Also attach the report** (off each time): ticked, the provider's report (see **Your part of this network**) is made once, as a PDF where the report format is PDF, else HTML, named `topo_<organization>_<site>_<date>_<provider>.pdf`; Email then copies the link, downloads the report once and opens the mail program, whose body says "Report: attached ({file})", and the step says to attach the file that just downloaded. Several providers get one report of all their parts. Through a Topo server that sends mail, the server attaches it (PDF only, 6 MB at most; otherwise it is downloaded for you to send), and the step warns: "The server sends the report unlocked, so the server (and whoever runs it) can read it. The link stays locked."
- **The client's side afterwards**: the full editor has a **Problems** tab (shown once the network has a report, its count the ones not closed), newest first: the status (Reported, Sent, Visited, Fixed), what and where (a click shows the device on the map), when, the client's words, who reported it, whom it went to, each visit (who, for which provider, when, how it went, the notes and, folded, what changed), when and why it was marked fixed, and **Send again**, **Mark fixed** (asks for an optional note; one undo step, a log entry) or **Reopen** (open again, one round up: a merge with an older closed copy cannot undo it). The Simple and imagenology views list the problems not closed (with nothing selected, all of them; with a device selected, its own) with Send (Send again once sent) and Mark fixed, and the map marks a device with a problem not closed with a small amber ! at its bottom-left corner (on a collapsed group or host when one is folded in), which no export carries. A network card on the landing says **⚠ Open problems: 2 · newest No internet, {date}**, and its ⚠ tool opens the network on its Problems tab. The site report has a **Problems and visits** tick, off by default because it prints the reporter's name and contact: each report with its visits, read from the network log by id (a visit not in this copy is said, not guessed). The network log tells the reports' and visits' entries in the reader's language.
- A network holds up to 200 reports; over that the oldest closed go first. There is no Delete: a report is closed, and history only grows.

## Provider visits

- **Arrival**: opening a link or a file whose network holds a report Reported or Sent shows a banner on the map, "Problem reported {date}: No internet at Reception PC." with **I am the provider** and **Not now** (remembered for that copy in this tab). Only an arrival shows it, never a network opened from your own library, the example or a template, and never a read-only one. A merge that brings a report not open here before (an IT firm that already keeps the client's network opens the client's file) shows it too.
- **I am the provider** opens provider mode: the full editor, borrowed for this tab (your Settings keep the view you had, and Leave provider mode gives it back), a strip at the top of the map ("⚠ No internet · Reception PC", **Record visit**, **Details**) and a **Problem report** tab: what, where (a click shows it), when, the client's words, who reported it with call and email buttons, **Which provider are you?** (the ones it was sent to first; remembered in this browser per network), what you look after here with **Show on map**, the visits so far, **Record visit** and **Leave provider mode**. The report's devices are ringed in amber on the map (never in an export). A reload of the tab comes back into provider mode; typing `#provider` does nothing else.
- **Your part of this network** (provider mode, under "You look after", translated): a report in the print preview (Print, Download PDF or HTML, A4), named `topo_<organization>_<site>_<date>_<provider>.pdf`: the site, the client and their contact, the provider's own record; this problem; their devices in full (model, serial, addresses, MAC, place, rack, ports and VLANs, and how they answer for each: named on it, through its host, through the group); the linked devices marked Hop 1, Hop 2, Runs their devices or Where the problem is, with the ports facing theirs and their addresses; the cables with ports at both ends; a diagram of only those; their other open problems and their own past visits. No findings (worked out on a part they would be wrong). **Devices CSV** and **Cables CSV** (`…_<provider>-devices.csv`, `…_<provider>-cables.csv`) carry their devices and the cables at them for their own records, in Topo's CSV layout (without the provider columns, which would name only them); read back into Topo they change nothing. The client gets the same report from the Providers tab, titled "{name}'s part of this network".
- The provider works on the network as usual. **Record visit** (translated): arrived and left (by default when the copy arrived, or when the last visit left, and now), how it went (**Fixed**, **Partly fixed**, **Not fixed**, **Needs a follow-up visit**), notes (up to 4000 characters), the technician's name (needed; the report's **Prepared by** name, and remembered as it), the client's name typed on this device as a sign-off (optional), which provider, and a read-only list of **what changed since the copy arrived** (devices, cables, subnets, Wi-Fi networks, groups, providers, columns and searches added, changed or removed; the reports and the log are not the network). A second visit in the same session lists what changed since the first went back. When the copy that arrived is no longer held in this tab, it says so and the visit saves without a list. Saving is one undo step: a `visit` entry in the network log (with the provider's name as it was then, since log entries are never renamed), the report Visited.
- **Sending it back**: a locked link with the passphrase that opened the incoming one (held in memory only, never stored: the client already has it, and the sheet says so; after a reload, or for a file, a new one shown to send another way), **Email the client** (to the reporter's address, subject "Visit recorded at {site}: {outcome}"; as the report's Email, the link goes to the clipboard as the hyperlink "Follow the infrastructure link" and the email has an "Infrastructure link:" line to paste under), a call button when the reporter left only a phone, **Share…**, **Copy message**, **Copy link**, and **Download** (`topo_<organization>_<site>_<date>_visit.json`). The copy names as its base the revision the provider received, so the client's merge is three-way.
- **The client takes it back** (docs/SCOPE.md D139, D153, phase 7b): every visit waits for the network's owner, and nothing a provider changed applies until they review it. Opening the returned link or file shows "{name} recorded a visit on {date}: Fixed." with **Review the visit**, also when a second provider's visit comes back for a report already visited; the copy is kept in **Visits waiting** at once (this browser, until decided, even if the tab closes). The merge review lists the report in plain words ("No internet at Reception PC. Now: Visited") and each visit the copy brings under the rows ("Visit by Juan Pérez (Fibra Norte), 9 Nov 2026, 14:10: Fixed. 2 changes."), and its foot asks two things: **the changes** (**Apply as reviewed**, or **Reject: apply nothing**) and **the visit** (**Successful**: the problem is marked fixed; **Not successful**: it opens again for another round, as Reopen does; **Undecided**: it stays as it is), with **A note for the provider**. The answer is a `decision` entry in the network log (format v19: "Visit by Juan Pérez (Fibra Norte) approved: Successful.", "… rejected: note"), in the same undo step as the merge; a rejected copy is downloaded as `…_rejected-visit.json`. Cancel leaves the copy in Visits waiting. **Visits waiting** (the Problems tab, the Simple view with nothing selected, "Visits waiting: 2" on the landing card): oldest first, the provider, the problem, the number of visits and changes, where it came from, **Review…**. One copy per provider and report: a newer copy for the same report replaces the waiting one (it holds the earlier visit too, "2 visits"). Two providers' copies are reviewed one after the other, each merged three-way against the network as the earlier approval left it; a field both changed asks, yours by default. A binder site with a visit is never merged on its own ("A visit to review").

## The Topo server

A client can host a Topo server (docs/SERVER.md, docs/SCOPE.md step 5a): one program that serves this same app and keeps networks in step between the client, their providers and anyone they invite, end-to-end encrypted. Served by anything else (topo.carino.systems, the offline zip, the desktop apps), the app is exactly as described above: the server code in it never starts and makes no request.

- **Signing in** (translated): **Sign in** in "On this server" on the landing asks for an email and sends a six-digit code, which signs in at the sixth digit, or a link that works in the browser that asked for it ("This link works in the browser where you asked for it" otherwise). The answer is the same whether or not the address may sign in. Too many tries say how many minutes to wait. **Send a new code** comes after 30 seconds.
- **Keys and the recovery key**: the first sign-in on a browser makes its keys and shows the recovery key once (28 letters and digits in groups of four) with Print and Download; owners must tick that they kept it, others may Skip. A second browser of the same person is opened by **Approve from another browser** (both show the same short code; the other browser approves it under Account) or **Use my recovery key**; **I lost both: reset my keys** makes new keys and each site's owners give access again. A private window keeps keys in memory only, and the account sheet says so.
- **Account** (the email in "On this server"): the language of the emails, six email choices (changes on sites you own, on sites shared with you, visits, problems sent to you, problems closed, someone waiting for access), the browsers (remove one, approve or deny a new one), sign out everywhere else, a new recovery key, reset my keys, sign out. The server owner also sees **People on this server** (add someone as staff, who may put networks on the server, or member; disable).
- **Put on the server** (☁ on a kept network's card, for staff and the owner): the network is encrypted and uploaded, and it moves into **On this server**; a question asks whether its name may appear in emails (No by default, with the warning that the host could read it). A site on the server that this browser has not opened shows as a card with **Open**; opening it decrypts it into a card here that syncs. A network this browser already has asks to merge with the server's copy.
- **Sync**: each server card says **Synced**, **Saving to the server…**, **Offline: {n} local changes**, **Needs merge: tap to review**, **Waiting for the owner to give you access**, **Open this account on this browser first**, **The server sent an older or different history**, or **You were removed from this site**; on a phone the document bar's chip folds to Synced, Saving…, Offline · 3 or Merge, and a tap on it opens the state with Sync now, Review merge, Members and the account. Edits save locally as always and go to the server after a few quiet seconds (at most a minute apart); offline they wait in the card. Changes from others arrive live: with nothing to merge they simply appear (one undo step, no log entry); with edits on both sides that do not collide Topo merges them quietly (one network log entry, "Merged the server"); when the same thing was changed on both sides the card says Needs merge and the usual merge review opens only when tapped, with a restore point first. A card is never pushed over a newer server copy.
- **Members** (👥 on a server card): every member with their role and safety code (24 digits). Owners invite by email with a role (provider by default) and the email's language, resend or cancel an invitation, **Give access** to someone who accepted (comparing their safety code, or without checking, marked "Not checked"; also the older history, ticked by default), change roles, **Remove** (a new key: they will not see new changes, what they already opened stays with them), **Rotate** after someone leaves, show the name in emails, mute this site's emails, and **History on the server** (each revision's number, author, time and size, read-only). Others can **Leave**. Someone whose keys changed is flagged in red and needs the code check.
- **Providers on the server**: a provider member opens the site with no link; their "who am I" is the provider whose email is theirs. A provider proposes, an owner approves (D139, D154): a provider's browser never pushes; its edits stay in its copy (its card: "Your changes go to the owner when you record a visit", the chip **Proposed**), and newer versions from the server still merge in under them. **Record visit** uploads a **visit copy** to the owners ("Sent to the owner for review. …"; the card: "Your visit is waiting for the owner's review", **With the owner**); a second one for the same report replaces it. The owners get the visit-recorded email and see it in **Visits waiting**, reviewed three-way against the revision the copy was made on; a change to anything the provider does not cover is marked "Outside what Fibra Norte covers." and left out unless **Apply it** is pressed. Once answered, the owner's result is pushed, then the answer sent; the provider gets an email ("The owner approved your visit" or "… did not accept your visit") and their copy takes the server's version, offering a rejected copy (or later edits the owner never got, and rows the owner left out) as a `…_my-visit.json` file first. If a later copy still carries what an earlier, rejected copy changed, the owner's review marks it "This change came in a visit you rejected before." and leaves it out unless applied (D191). A provider removed from the site, or made a viewer or owner, takes their waiting copies with them. A Topo older than 0.0.2 that still pushes as a provider is refused ("Only owners save versions of this site", update Topo). **Send** on a report routed to providers who are all members says "Sent through the server. {names} will get an email." instead of making a link; an owner's own visit recorded in provider mode is "Saved to the server. The client will get an email." Sent to providers who are not all members, the report is a locked link as without a server and, on a press of **✉ Send by email**, **the server emails it** to them (with the report attached as a PDF when **Also attach the report** is ticked) ("Sent by email from the server to {names}. Send the passphrase another way, such as a call or a text."; From the server, Reply-To the client, the link as "Follow the infrastructure link"); a server with no SMTP, or one that refuses (too many reports for now), opens the mail program instead and says why. A viewer's copy is read-only.
- **Emails** (in each person's language, no network content): the sign-in code, invitations, someone waiting for access, access given, a problem sent to you (its link reads "Follow the infrastructure link"), a visit recorded, a problem closed, a digest of changes (at most one per site per hour; on by default for sites you own), a new browser asking to open your account. The site's name appears only if an owner allowed it.

- **Backups** (docs/SCOPE.md 5b and 5c, D99-D111): off until the server owner turns them on in **Account, Backups…**: a folder on the server, WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud, Synology, QNAP), an S3-compatible bucket (Backblaze B2, Wasabi, MinIO, AWS; a path-style tick for MinIO), or Google Drive or OneDrive (5c, D109-D111: the client's own app's ID and secret, then **Connect** opens the provider's sign-in page and comes back "Connected as …"; Topo asks only for its own folder, and the sign-in is kept on the server, never in the browser), a daily time, Save (tests the destination first; "Save anyway?" for a disk not plugged in yet), Test, Back up now, Check now, the status in words, each network's backup count and **Open the latest backup here** (opens it for real), deleted networks' backups (Download the latest, Delete). Passwords are sent once and shown as "Saved (type to replace)". The server writes each network's encrypted copy (it cannot read it) a few minutes after changes and daily, and keeps the last day, then one a day for a week, a week for a month, a month for a year. Each network's owners see its files in **Members, Backups…**: **Restore…** (unlocked with this browser's keys, checked, then Merge, Replace with this copy after a restore point "Before restoring a backup", or Open as a copy; logged as "a backup"; pushed as one new revision on top) and **Download**. A backup file opened anywhere else (Open a file, any Topo, no server needed) asks for the recovery key of one of its owners, lists the owners who have none, and says a wrong or older key in words; a changed file, a member list this browser does not trust, or a newer format is refused in words; an author whose keys changed since opens with a note in the review. **Keep a readable copy too** (only when the server owner allows readable copies, after a question saying who could read it): an owner's browser sends the readable export after its syncs, at most every 10 minutes; not end-to-end encrypted, and only as recent as an owner's last visit; turning it off offers to delete the copies made.

## Offline and desktop

- **The offline zip** is for desktop browsers: unzip it and double-click `index.html` (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari). Nothing is installed and no connection is needed. Saved networks stay in that browser, for that folder. Share links and label QR codes point at topo.carino.systems, since a file on one computer opens nowhere else; the Topo name in the top bar opens the folder, so use Back to return.
- **Windows** (x64, Arm): an installer (`-setup.exe`; the `.msi` is no longer built, D125), using the system's WebView2; the installer carries WebView2's offline installer and puts it in when it is missing, so it needs no internet (an intranet PC installs it as it is).
- **macOS** (Apple silicon, Intel): a `.dmg`, signed ad hoc only; Print opens the report in the default browser, which prints it.
- **Linux** (x64, Arm): `.deb`, `.rpm` or `.AppImage`.
- **The apps open on a short intro**, as Carino DICOM's does: the map Topo draws (an uplink, a router, a switch and three devices) drawn in, a packet running down to each device, and the name in gold, in Gold or Colourful (light or dark) as the app was last left and in its language, about two seconds, then the editor. A click or a key skips it; with reduced motion it is a still frame for half a second. It is `desktop/intro.html`, staged only into the desktop apps (never the zip or the website), and the editor replaces it, so Back never returns to it.
- The apps keep their library in their own data folder, apart from the website's; save files to Downloads; open links to the web in the default browser. A network not kept is lost when the app quits.
- **This PC as the server** (the desktop apps only; docs/SCOPE.md D113, D125, docs/SERVER.md "Install"): one package per computer, and the PC it is on can become the server for the others. The Windows installer's first page has **Use this PC as the server for your network** (silent installs: `/SERVER`); on Linux (rpm, deb) and macOS, Topo asks once on its first start. Then the server runs as a service from startup, even with Topo closed and nobody signed in, and Settings, **This PC as the server**, talks to it: whether it runs, the addresses to give people, the fingerprint, its log, Save, Start and Stop through an administrator prompt, and **Stop using this PC as the server** (the data stays). The other way stays too: the app runs the server itself, only while Topo is open (the AppImage can only do this); **Start it when Topo opens** starts it with the app. **This PC only** runs at `http://127.0.0.1:7380` (another port if asked); sign-in codes can go by email or show in the section's log. **Other PCs on this network** runs over https with a certificate the PC makes for itself, covering all its addresses: the section lists each address (Copy) and the certificate's fingerprint, which each other PC compares once when its browser warns. Sign-in codes then go by email (the SMTP fields; the password is kept by the app and shown as "Saved (type to replace)"). The firewall may ask to let topo-server in; the PC needs a fixed address, since sign-in links carry it. **Open it in this window** makes the app's own window a client of that server (below), at 127.0.0.1 and pinned to the certificate the PC made. A log shows what the server says.
- **Connect to a Topo server** (the desktop apps only; docs/SCOPE.md D144, docs/SERVER.md "The desktop app as a client"): Settings takes a server's address, this PC's own or another's, and the app's window then keeps networks on it as a browser on that address would: sign in by the emailed code, keys, sync, live events, Visits waiting. Every request and the event stream go through the app (Rust), never from the page, so the page's CSP and the server stay as they are; the session stays in the app across restarts. A server that signs its own certificate shows its fingerprint to compare once and is pinned to it; a different certificate later is refused and said, with **Check the certificate again**. A server whose certificate stops being trusted is refused the same way. Plain http only to this PC. In the app the email sign-in is by its code, and Google Drive or OneDrive backups are connected from a browser on the server. **Disconnect** signs out and forgets the server. The website and the zip show nothing of this.
- **Add this computer** (the desktop apps only; docs/SCOPE.md D166, D204-D212): in the Nodes panel and in Settings, **This computer**. The app reads this PC's own interfaces (names, MACs, IPv4 and IPv6 addresses with prefixes, MTU), its default gateways and its DNS servers, from the PC alone (nothing is sent onto the network), and the Import… review shows what it understood: this PC as a device (its name, its system, its DNS servers), its connected network cards with one address each (IPv4 first, else a stable IPv6) and the subnets of those addresses. The default gateway is not added; the review shows it, with the MTUs, any address not kept and what was left out, as information. Virtual interfaces (bridges, containers, tunnels) are left out unless ticked. Nothing is kept before the merge review, and running it again updates the same device. The website and the zip show nothing of this.
- **The Topo server's own installers** (docs/SERVER.md "Install"; D112, D125): not offered to clients, who install the desktop app; the recipes stay for an advanced host: an rpm and a deb with a systemd unit (not started until set up), a Windows service (`topo-server service install`) with a WiX recipe for an MSI, and a macOS LaunchDaemon with a pkg recipe. Not signed yet.
- **Phones and tablets** use the website: iOS Files and Android cannot run a local `index.html`, and there is no mobile app.

## Answer checks, monitors and statistics (step 7)

docs/SCOPE.md section 7 (D126 onwards), built in phases 7a to 7m. What it
changes in Topo's promise:

- **Still not a scanner.** Topo never discovers anything: no sweeps, ARP, SNMP, LLDP or port scans, and nothing that reads another device's configuration.
- **An answer check, from the desktop app only.** A desktop app that someone turns into a monitor (**Monitor from this PC**, off as shipped) asks the devices and services already documented, and marked to be checked, one question: "Are you answering?" Ping, TCP, HTTP(S), DNS, SMTP (the greeting only, nothing sent), DICOM C-ECHO, NTP, RTSP, SIP, HL7 MLLP (a connect only), IPP, IMAP and POP. Each check is one fixed request to an address and port already in the document, and keeps only whether it answered, how long it took and the reply's status code. The website and the offline zip never probe; signed in to a Topo server, they show the status, the issues and the reports.
- **Monitors.** A site monitor (turned on by an owner on a Topo server, permanent: **Site monitors** below) or a visit monitor (a provider's, for one visit, its figures going into the visit report: **Monitor during this visit** below). Without a server, one desktop app monitors on its own (**Monitor from this PC** below).
- **Issues and alarms.** An outage becomes an issue that stays open until someone acknowledges it, reports it to the provider or marks it expected. Alarms are on screen only (**Show alarms on this PC**, off as shipped): a full-screen card on the desktop app, a banner on signed-in pages. No email, call, SMS or push (**Issues and the alarm** below).
- **Statistics and reports.** Response times and availability, collected automatically while checks run and kept 360 days by default (changed or turned off in Settings): availability with its coverage, response times, outages with MTTR and MTBF, and a provider report, through the report preview with CSV (**Monitoring report** below).
- **The rest of step 7:** the merge fixes (sent parts kept until answered, another provider's new devices never joined by name alone, ids with a random part), provider visits through the owner (**The client takes it back**, **Providers on the server**), **Connect to a Topo server**, **Add this computer**, **The screen lock** and **The welcome**.

## The welcome

The first time Topo starts on a PC or in a browser, a plain start with nothing open (never a shared link, a `#setup` or `?profile=` intake, `#provider` or a mode entry such as `#simple`), a window asks a few questions, one per step. Every step has **Skip this step**, and ✕ or Esc skips the rest; skipped, nothing changes. **Next** applies the step: the look (Gold or Colourful, kept as the navbar's switch keeps it); the view (**Document the network**, the full editor, or **Report problems**, the simple view for General or Imagenology, as the mode picker sets it; the next plain start opens the full editor again, as always); where networks are kept (the desktop app: **On this PC only**, **Connect to a Topo server**, or **Make this PC the server** where the app can run it and has not asked before; both server choices end the welcome in Settings at that section; the website: this browser, and a Topo server opens at its own address; a page a Topo server served does not ask); the screen lock's time and an optional PIN; in the desktop app, **Add this computer to the map** and **Monitor from this PC**, both off unless ticked and done once a network is open (the review, and the monitor's offer in Settings); then **Open a file**, **Map the network now** (Add devices, as `#setup` opens it) or **An empty network**. It shows once per PC or browser; a browser or app that already holds networks is not asked. Settings, **Run the welcome again**, shows it any time.

## The screen lock

After 30 minutes without use (Settings, **Screen lock**: Off, 5, 15, 30, 60 min or a custom time, for this PC or browser only) Topo saves the open network, any Report a problem or Record visit being written, and a server card's pending changes, then closes the network and covers the screen. Every Topo tab in that browser locks with it, and one opened while locked opens locked. **Lock now** locks at once. Unlock with a PIN set on this PC (4 to 12 digits, kept only as a slow hash) or by signing in to your Topo server again with a code sent to your email; the network and the dialog being written come back as they were. Each wrong PIN says how many of its 10 tries are left, and after 10 only the sign-in unlocks. Where there is no server to sign in to (the website, the offline zip, an app that is not a client), **Forget the PIN** appears from the fifth wrong PIN and unlocks and removes it, which Settings then says, and after 10 each further try waits longer. If your server does not answer, the PIN still works (with the same waits) and **Forget the PIN** and the plain **Unlock** never appear, so cutting the network is not a way in. Once it has not answered for 72 hours, the one way past the sign-in is **Forget the network and unlock**: the network that was open and every network from that server kept on the PC are deleted from it and the window signs out, so the screen opens on none of them (they stay on the server; changes not sent yet are lost). Nothing on the cover or in the note names the network. With no PIN and no sign-in a plain **Unlock** opens it, and the lock only hides the screen. While locked, no key or paste reaches the network under the cover. The lock stops someone using a screen left unattended. It is not encryption: the networks stay in the browser's storage as before, readable by anyone who can use the PC's account and the browser's own tools.

## Monitor from this PC

The desktop app only, off as shipped (Settings, **Monitor from this PC**). It monitors one kept network: turning it on offers **Keep Topo in the system tray when the window is closed** and **Open Topo at sign-in**, both ticked, so checking goes on with the window closed. Each device can carry up to 8 answer checks (the device panel's **Answer checks**): ping (a TCP connect labelled "TCP, not ping" where this PC may not ping), TCP, HTTP(S) (the status code and the certificate's end date, warned at 14 days), DNS (one name the owner gives), SMTP (greeting, EHLO, optional STARTTLS, QUIT; only where the device documents an SMTP port; never a login or a message) DICOM C-ECHO (called and calling AE titles per check), NTP (one time request: the stratum and this PC's clock offset, warned above 1 s), RTSP (OPTIONS, the status code only), SIP (OPTIONS over UDP or TCP, the status code only), HL7 MLLP (a connect only: no message is sent, so nothing can reach a RIS), IPP (the printer's state and its reasons only, never a print job; a stopped printer is warned) and IMAP and POP (the greeting, optionally STARTTLS or TLS from the start for the certificate's end date, then LOGOUT or QUIT; never a login). A printer, a camera and a phone system are offered IPP, RTSP and SIP first when they document 631, 554 and 5060. Every 60 s by default (30 s to 15 min), at most 8 at once. A device is down after 3 failures and up after 2 answers; slow, flapping, quiet (maintenance for an hour, a morning or a day, on a device, its group or the whole site) and unknown are shown too, and a device behind one that is down says so. The map shows a dot on each checked device (no export carries it), and the section shows the last 24 h. Samples stay 7 days on this PC and hourly figures follow **Keep statistics** (360 days; Off keeps nothing), in a file readable by this user only. Nothing is discovered: each check is one fixed request to an address and port already in the document.

## Issues and the alarm

On the PC that monitors, a device that stops answering on its own (nothing upstream of it down) becomes an **issue**: one per outage, naming the devices behind it ("Router R1 and 12 devices behind it"), when it was first seen down and which PC saw it. It goes into the network's **Problems** (the Problems tab, the Simple view's list, the map's marker and the landing card's line) as soon as that network is open in an editable window, with a line in the network log. If the device answers again the issue says "answering again since" in a softer colour, and stays open; if it goes down again while open, the outage is added to the same issue. It closes only when someone acts: **Acknowledge** (with a note if you like), **Report a problem** (the usual report, prefilled with the device and what the monitor saw, routed to its provider; nothing is sent until you send it) or **Expected** (maintenance or planned work: its time counts as maintenance in the statistics, and the device shows quiet until it answers again). Owners act; a provider only on the devices they cover; a viewer only sees. Two people acting at once keep both acts.

**Show alarms on this PC** (Settings, off as shipped) adds the alarm: when an issue opens, a full-screen amber card on every screen of the PC, above other windows, says NOT ANSWERING, the device, how long, what is behind it and "Checked by monitor <PC>", with a short chime (the volume is left as it is) and **Acknowledge**, **Report a problem** and **Later** (Escape too; the issue stays open). A system notification goes out too, since on Wayland a window cannot always stay on top, and with several screens the card may show on one of them only. A device answering again is a small notification only. While Topo is locked the card still names the device; Acknowledge and Report a problem ask to unlock first. **Test the alarm** shows the card marked DRILL. No email, call, SMS or push.

## Site monitors (through a Topo server)

On a network kept on a Topo server (docs/SERVER.md), an owner can turn a PC with the desktop app into the site's **monitor** (Settings, under Monitor from this PC: **Make this PC the site's monitor**, and the groups it checks; none is the whole site). The PC's own key goes into its keychain (or, where the system has none, a file only that user can read, and Settings says so), and from then on it checks and reports with no one signed in: its status at least once a minute, its issues and its hourly figures, each encrypted with the network's key and signed by that PC. Members lists it as "Monitor: PC name (turned on by X on date)" with its last report, and an owner can **Revoke** it (the network's key changes for everyone left, the other monitors included). Each monitor checks the devices in its groups; a check can name the monitors that ask it (**Checked by**), two of them to compare: "not answering from A, answering from B" points at the path between them, not the device.

Every page signed in to the server, the website included, sees the monitors' work: the map's dots, the device's state by monitor, and a banner at the top for an open issue or for a monitor that stopped reporting (3 missed minutes; an app with Show alarms on also gets a notification). The next owner's page that has the network open puts the issues into Problems; acts on them go to the server as signed records too, so the monitor stops raising the alarm. A record that is not signed by a member allowed to write it (only a monitor opens an issue; a provider acts only on the devices they cover) is dropped and shown to the owners. **Keep statistics** belongs to the site (Members, Settings: 360 days; Off keeps nothing anywhere), and issues not yet in the document go into the server's backups, so a restore loses none.

## Monitoring report

**📈 Monitoring report** (the file menu, under Site report), a device's **📈 Full report…** (its Answer checks) and a provider card's **Monitoring report** open the report in the print preview, with its choices in the left column: the whole network, a group, a device or a provider; the last 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, 91 days (a quarter) or since the last visit; for a provider, around the clock or only during their support hours; and what goes in. **Availability** per check, device, group and provider: the time answering over the time answering and not answering, with maintenance and unknown time left out and how much of the period was measured printed beside every figure; the time a device was down only because a device upstream was is named ("down because R1") and left out of the figure its target is judged on (the device's own target, else its provider's, which the provider card now holds, with "Judge the target only during their support hours"). **Response times**: per check the median and 95th percentile over the period as a chart with its table beside it, the jitter, the loss and the slow hours of the day; a ping this PC could only ask over TCP says "TCP, not ping". **Outages**: each with its start, how long it was down, its root cause, who acted and how long they took to acknowledge, and per device the mean time to repair (MTTR) and between failures (MTBF), Expected outages counting as maintenance. **Print**, **Download** (PDF or HTML) and **CSV** (one file, a row per check, step and outage). The figures come from the site's monitors through a Topo server (any signed-in page, the website too) or, in the desktop app with no server, from this PC's own monitor of the open network; without either the report says so and still lists the outages. A provider's own report (and the one a problem report attaches) carries a Monitoring section on their devices since their last visit, and the network report's Problems and visits prints each outage with its times down and who acted.

## Monitor during this visit

In provider mode, on the desktop app, the report's tab offers **Monitor during this visit** once you have chosen which provider you are. It asks the answer checks of the devices that provider looks after (a device with none is not asked: add one in its **Answer checks**, which becomes part of the visit's changes), on this PC and under your own sign-in or none: no monitor key, nothing sent to a server while it runs. The tab and the map show each check's state, and an outage seen is said on your screen; the client gets no issue, card or notification from it, since work in progress takes devices down on purpose. **Stop** ends the checks and drops the samples but keeps the figures; **Record visit** adds them to the visit: per check, how many answered, the loss, the availability with how much of the visit was measured, the median and 95th-percentile response time, a chart, and the outages seen with the device upstream they were down because of. When the site has a monitor whose figures your page can read (a provider member on a Topo server, or this PC monitoring the network), each check is compared with that monitor's 7 days before the visit ("the median went from 38 ms to 4 ms"). The section travels with the visit (in the link or file back, or in the visit copy the owner reviews), prints in the visit's own **Visit report** (from the dialog once the visit is saved) and under the visit in the network report. The samples are kept in memory for the session only; everything goes at Record visit, at Discard, when a new session starts, or when you sign out of the server. With statistics off (the site's **Keep statistics**, or this PC's), the states still show, nothing is kept and the visit gets no monitoring section.

## Privacy

Topo has no server of its own and uploads nothing (a Topo server the client hosts is the one exception: see The Topo server above and docs/SERVER.md; what reaches it is encrypted). Kept networks live in this browser's storage (IndexedDB, falling back to local storage where IndexedDB is not offered), and the network you are working on is held in the page's history, not in the URL. The comparison copy behind "changes since the last back-up" and the address history noted at each back-up and report are kept the same way, beside the card, and never leave this browser, except that a report you tick **Address history** on prints that network's history (off by default). A share link carries the whole network (or, for a problem report or **Share their part**, only a provider's part: docs/SCOPE.md D91-D92) and is locked with a passphrase by default (AES-GCM). A report attached by a Topo server's email goes unlocked through that server, which the step says. The suggested passphrase is masked in the share dialog until **Show** reveals it; **Copy passphrase** copies it after the link. A link that does not read as a network (cut short, mangled, or unlocked to something that is not one) says it is damaged and asks for it to be sent again: the network already open stays as it was, kept card and all, and on a fresh visit Topo opens as a bare visit would. Before a network leaves as a link or a report, Topo names any device whose notes look like they hold a password. A Wi-Fi network has no passphrase field, so a Wi-Fi key cannot travel in a file, a link or a sheet. The network log does travel, in files and links (see **Keeping your work**): each entry names a file only by its name (never a folder), and who did it only by Settings' Prepared by name, when one is set. The revisions kept to find a returning copy's base stay in this browser, like the comparison copy, and a network nobody kept leaves none behind. Problem reports, visits and the network log travel with the network: in its files, in its links (a link carries the newest 50 log entries; the whole log comes back through a merge) and, when their ticks are on, in its reports, and a report holds the reporter's name, phone and email. A report is only ever sent locked or as a file, never as an unlocked link; the share dialog leaves the reports and their log entries out unless **Include problem reports and visits** is ticked (off each time). Provider mode keeps the copy that arrived, reporter included, in this tab's session storage until the tab closes, and the passphrase that opened it in memory only. The log's chain shows an edit made outside Topo that did not recompute it; it is not a signature. Topo makes no outside requests at all: its Content-Security-Policy allows connections to this site only; the email, call and share buttons hand a `mailto:`, a `tel:` or the system's share sheet to this device, which is not a request.

## Scale

A 300-device site (a fixture of one ships with the tests) redraws, checks itself, fills the table and previews a bulk change in tens of milliseconds, and a test holds it to a time budget.
